   The one sentence to hold onto

   The tech that pairs best with criminology isn't a specific AI product — it's research and
   investigation skills applied to digital evidence: OSINT, structured analysis, SQL, and
   enough Python to clean data. That combination is what Innefu, Kroll, CloudSEK, and I4C are
   actually hiring for. Crypto and AI red-teaming are worth knowing about — one as a later
   specialisation, one as pocket money — but neither is where you point six months of full-time
   study.

   How to actually use this

   Don't spend six months or any money chasing "blockchain investigator" as a job title in India
   — the postings barely exist, and the ones that do want 1–3 years' experience plus SQL and
   Python. Instead: learn the free tools this month (a weekend, not a course), reproduce two or
   three publicly documented crypto-crime cases from Rekt.news as portfolio pieces, and use
   that as a talking point inside an AML/fraud analyst application — which is a real, high-volume
   job (see the first guide). Crypto becomes your specialisation inside that job in year two, once
   you have SQL and Python and can credibly apply for the vendor-side investigator roles that
   actually require them.

DOOR THREE


OSINT and crime-tech: the
one to actually build toward
This is where your instinct was right, just aimed slightly wide. Not "AI jobs" —
investigation and analysis jobs that increasingly run on digital tools, at
companies that already sell to Indian police and corporate clients.



   1      OSINT Analyst, Innefu Labs            VERIFIED · FRESHER-ELIGIBLE

          Delhi · 0–1 years experience

          A genuinely verified posting from an Indian AI/OSINT firm that sells to law enforcement. Asked for social-media fluency and communication skill, not
          an engineering degree. This is the single clearest match for your exact profile found anywhere in this research — watch their careers page and set
          a LinkedIn alert.



   2      Business Intelligence Analyst, Kroll              REAL, LIVE POSTING

          Mumbai · Analyst I tier

          Kroll's Business Intelligence and Investigations practice does corporate due diligence and integrity research — reading public filings, litigation
          records, and media to build a picture of a person or company. This is investigative research work, and it is exactly what a criminology degree trains
          you to do. Pinkerton runs similar Intelligence Analyst postings globally, sometimes open to India.



   3      I4C Analyst — 195-post recruitment                CONTRACT, 3-YEAR, WATCH FOR NEXT CYCLE

          ₹65,000/month (~₹7.8 LPA) · Ministry of Home Affairs

          This actually happened in May 2026 — 195 contractual posts at India's Cybercrime Coordination Centre, including 57 Analyst-tier roles at
          ₹65,000/month, alongside Consultant and Senior Consultant posts. It's contractual, not permanent, and the cycle has closed — but it confirms MHA
          runs real, paid, periodic analyst-tier recruitment. Register for job alerts on i4c.mha.gov.in and check back.



   4      SOC Analyst, fresher tier          HIGHEST VOLUME, WEAKEST FIT

          ₹2.5–4.5 LPA · Sequretek and similar firms run explicit fresher postings

          The most plentiful technical-adjacent entry point in India, and Sequretek has a verified "SOC Analyst – Fresher" role on its own careers portal.
          Honest caveat: this is fundamentally a cybersecurity monitoring job — logs, alerts, SIEM tools — not a criminology-flavoured role. It needs basic
          networking fundamentals, which the roadmap below covers, but don't expect your degree to matter much here. Use it as a fallback, not a first
          choice.



   5      Cyber Threat Intelligence Analyst, CloudSEK                    SMALL BUT REAL, INTERNSHIP FUNNEL EXISTS

          Bengaluru · verified internship-to-hire posting on Greenhouse

          CloudSEK does dark-web monitoring and digital risk protection and has a documented internship pipeline. The market for pure threat-intel analyst
          roles in India is small — dozens, not hundreds, of genuinely fresher-open postings — but it exists and is criminology-adjacent (reading forums,
          tracking threat actors, writing intelligence reports).



   ✕      Digital forensics practitioner          CLOSED WITHOUT A BRIDGE CREDENTIAL


          The honest finding: this field is gatekept by CS/IT/forensic-science degrees and expensive certifications (CHFI runs well over ₹25,000). A
          criminology-only graduate is not a natural fit for the core technical forensic work in India — you'd need an additional forensic-science or CS bridge
          qualification first. Rule this out as a near-term target.



   ✕      "Crime Analyst" as an Indian job title             DOESN'T REALLY EXIST HERE


          In the US and UK, police departments directly employ civilian crime analysts for hotspot mapping and pattern analysis. In India, this function barely
          exists as a standing civilian role — it's absorbed into police-tech vendor "business analyst" posts or NGO research roles instead. Naukri's "793
          crime analyst jobs" figure is dominated by US/UK remote postings and financial-crime analyst roles wrongly tagged. Don't search this exact title.



   ✕      CCTNS / NATGRID / ICJS contractor roles                     EFFECTIVELY ENGINEERING-ONLY


          These national criminal-justice IT systems are built and staffed by Wipro, TCS, C-DAC, and NIC almost entirely through software-engineering and
          systems-integration hiring. No domain-analyst hiring pipeline was found. Not worth six months of study aimed here.



   ✕      MHA "Cyber Crime Volunteer" programme                       NOT A JOB — UNPAID, AND CONTROVERSIAL


          This is real, but it is an unpaid volunteer scheme for flagging content, not employment, and it drew significant civil-liberties criticism including a PIB
          clarification. Do not plan around it as income.

   The budget

   Google Data Analytics Certificate, one fast month (~₹3,000) · TryHackMe Premium, two
   months (~₹2,000–3,000) · an optional OSINT paid course if the free material feels thin
   (~₹2,500–3,500) · an optional SWAYAM certificate exam fee (~₹1,200) · a small buffer. Total:
   roughly ₹12,000–14,700. Everything else in this plan — Excel, SQL, Python basics, Power
   BI, Tableau, Bellingcat's OSINT toolkit, Trace Labs, the legal codes — is free. Skip PL-300
   (₹14,000+ alone), Security+ (₹34,000+), and GIAC's GOSI ($999) entirely; none of them are
   necessary at fresher level, and each alone would blow the budget.
