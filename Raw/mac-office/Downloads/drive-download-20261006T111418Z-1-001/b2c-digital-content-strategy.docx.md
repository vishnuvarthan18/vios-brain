1.5 SUMMARY
This document defines the digital content strategy for araCreate Academy's parent-facing audience, supporting the planning and publishing of consistent, engaging content for its evening and weekend AI, Robotics and Innovation programmes. It establishes the core messaging framework, content pillars, terminology standards, and publishing guidance to ensure all content produced by the Digital Marketing Team aligns with araCreate Academy's brand positioning. This is an internal working document intended to guide content creation and maintain consistency across all parent-facing communication.
2 AUDIENCE & POSITIONING
2.1 TARGET AUDIENCE
Parents of children roughly aged 8–16
Parents concerned about excessive screen time and passive device use
Parents actively looking for hands-on, skill-building extracurricular activities
Parents who want their child exposed to AI, robotics and real-world building — not just academics

What They Care About:

Will this genuinely reduce my child's screen time, or is it “more screens” in disguise?
Is this safe, structured, and run by people who know how to train kids, not just engineers talking to engineers?
Will my child actually enjoy it and stick with it?
Is this worth the time and money compared to other weekend activities (sports, music, tuition)?
2.2 TONE OF VOICE
Warm, encouraging, parent-to-parent not corporate or technical
Show real kids, real excitement, real outcomes avoid stock-photo energy
Lead with the child's experience and growth, not the technology specs
It is fine to be playful here unlike the B2B audience, this one responds well to warmth and fun

4  CONTENT STRATEGY
4.1  5 - PART CONTENT PILLAR FRAMEWORK
Unlike the 10-part B2B structure, parent content works best around five recurring pillars, rotated weekly. Each pillar answers a different parent concern.
4.1.1  THE SCREEN TIME PROBLEM
Acknowledge the real concern of too much passive screen time and position hands-on building as the answer. This pillar earns trust by naming the problem honestly.
4.1.2  WHAT KIDS ACTUALLY BUILD
Show real projects  robots, circuits, small AI projects  made by real kids in the class. This is the most persuasive content type for this audience.
4.1.3  THE LEARNING JOURNEY
Explaining the progression from basics to building to real challenges helps parents see this as a structured path, not a random activity.
4.1.4  WHO'S TRAINING THEM
Introduce our trainers as experienced engineers and technologists, not just instructors.” Builds the same credibility as the B2B content, in warmer language.
4.1.5  CLASS INFO & CALL TO ACTION
Schedule, location, age groups, how to enrol. Every week should include at least one clear, simple CTA post.

7 OPERATIONAL FRAMEWORK
7.1 PUBLISHING CADENCE
Publish 4–5 posts per week across primary channels (Instagram, Facebook and YouTube Shorts where applicable)
Publish at least 2–3 Reels per week, prioritising authentic classroom moments and student activities
Share one parent testimonial or student success story every month
Refresh class schedules, enrolment information and programme details whenever new batches are announced
7.2 REVIEW PROCESS
All captions reviewed against the Terminology Guide (Section 5) before scheduling
Any post featuring a child’s name, face or project requires confirmed parent/guardian consent before publishing
Verify all programme details (age groups, timings, venue and contact information) before publishing promotional content
Bashith/Navaneethan to review and approve monthly content batches before publishing
7.3 PRE-PUBLISH CHECKLIST
Before publishing, ensure every post:

Uses authentic photos or videos from araCreate Academy wherever possible
Focuses on the child's learning experience rather than technical specifications
Maintains a warm, encouraging and parent-friendly tone
Includes a clear Call to Action (CTA) where appropriate
Follows the approved terminology and branding guidelines
Protects student privacy by confirming all required permissions before publication
