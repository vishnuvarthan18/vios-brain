## Verification status — READ BEFORE USING ANY OF THIS ON THE SITE

**Nothing below has been independently checked yet.** This is one AI's
research pass, not a confirmed source. Per this site's own standing rule
(every claim carries a named source; nothing gets published without one),
none of these facts, citations, or superlatives should go on `sathyamangalam.org`

resolves, the paper says what's claimed, the source is what it claims to be.

## Prompt 1 — Endemism & biological uniqueness

**Verdict per the response itself: no species are strictly endemic to STR alone.**
STR functions as a connective corridor, not an isolated pocket, so any rare
species found here also occur in adjacent landscapes (MM Hills, BRT, wider
Nilgiri Biosphere). The response is explicit and consistent about this —
worth noting the AI did NOT try to oversell uniqueness here, which is a good
sign for its reliability on this prompt.

Three near-matches surfaced, all flagged by the source itself as *not*
strictly endemic:

1. **Abraham's Luisia** (*Luisia abrahami*) — epiphytic orchid.
   Cited: Prakash, L. & Balasubramanian, P. (2016), *Indian Journal of
   Forestry*, 39(3). Claimed as a Western Ghats endemic with STR as one of
   its easternmost confirmed locations, not exclusive to STR.

2. **Ganeshaiah's Dwarf Gecko** (*Cnemaspis ganeshaiahi*).
   Cited: Agarwal, I., et al. (2023), *Vertebrate Zoology* 73:189–203,
   DOI: 10.3897/vz.73.e101311. Described from Keeranhola Village in the MM
   Hills, bordering STR to the north — not described from inside STR itself.

3. **Menon's Barklouse** (*Hemipsocus menoni*) — insect.
   Cited: Ramesh, G., et al. (2025), *Zootaxa* 5575(3). STR (Pellarikovil
   pallam / Hasanur) given as a type locality, but the same species was also
   found in Palamu Tiger Reserve, Jharkhand — not restricted to STR.

**Strategic framing the response offered:** STR's real distinction is as a
genetic bridge/corridor between the Ghats, not a haven of point-endemics —
useful as an honest positioning angle, not a "found nowhere else" claim.

## Prompt 3 — Historical & archival firsts

Three items, each with an archive named:

1. **The Mackenzie Manuscripts** (late 18th/early 19th c.) — Colin
   Mackenzie's surveys covering the Kongu–Mysore plateau corridor, including
   Sathyamangalam/Dharapuram/Dimbam skirmishes and caste genealogies.
   Archive: Government Oriental Manuscripts Library (GOML), Chennai, and the
   British Library India Office Records. Full folios need institutional
   request.

2. **"The Mammals of the Coimbatore District"** (Rev. F.W. Jackson, 1875),
   nested inside Nicholson's 1887 *Manual of the Coimbatore District*
   (already a cited source on `history.html`). Claimed detail: leopards in
   Vellamundi mistakenly recorded as cheetahs; Nilgai along the Bhavani;
   Captain Caulfeild appointed "tiger-slayer" in 1873 along the
   Bhavani–Moyar tracts. Archive: Tamil Nadu State Archives; says a digitized
   copy is on the Internet Archive — **check this, it's the easiest item in
   this whole file to verify** since it should be a direct, checkable link.

3. **ARE No. 193 of 1927** — ASI epigraphy record, Jalakanthesvara Temple,
   Kaveripuram (Bhavani Taluk): a Kongu-era Vikrama-Chola land endowment
   inscription. Kaveripuram was later submerged by the Stanley Reservoir
   (Mettur Dam), making this transcription the only surviving record of an
   inscription that no longer physically exists — genuinely distinctive if
   verified. Source cited: *South Indian Inscriptions, Volume 19*, ASI
   Epigraphy Branch, Mysore.

4. **Two named Anthropological Survey of India studies** on Soliga/Irula/
   Kurumba/Kattunaickan communities across the Nilgiris–Wayanad–
   Sathyamangalam belt — Sathyanarayanan, C.R. & Chandra, N. (2000–2001,
   published 2012–13) and a companion "The Irular of Tamil Nadu" study by the
   same author. Both named, with institution (AnSI, Southern Regional
   Centre, Mysore) and stated publication venue.

## Prompt 4 — Cultural / community distinctiveness

Compares STR's Soliga/Irula communities against the better-documented BR
Hills Soliga (their Biligiri Ranganathaswamy Temple relationship, 64
recognized Podus) to isolate what's specifically STR's own.

**Ethnobotanical knowledge, STR-specific:**
- Poongodi, et al. (2011), *"Observations on some ethnomedicinal plants in
  Sathyamangalam"* — documents Irula/Soliga use of *Cucumis sativus*
  (cucumber pulp, applied to the abdomen for urinary trouble) and *Cynodon
  dactylon* (Bermuda grass extract, taken orally for dysentery/nosebleeds).
  Named study, specific formulations — checkable.
- A separate Hasanur Hills survey (source not fully named in the response —
  needs identifying before use) records *Flacourtia ramontchi*, *Zyzyphus
  oenoplia*, and *Hemidesmus indicus* root as staple wild dietary
  supplements, with leaves at 45% of medicinal preparations.

**Socio-political practices, contrasted with BR Hills:**
- **Driskell, M.K. (2018), *Current Conservation*** — FRA implementation
  study. Claims STR village leaders deliberately kept Forest Rights
  Committees (FRCs) "incompetent" to protect the older Village Forest
  Councils (VFCs) as their real interface with the Forest Department — the
  opposite of the usual tribal-advocacy position of championing the FRC.
  Named author, named publication, specific and checkable claim.
- **Iyer, A. (2015)** — report on Thalavadi/STR human-wildlife conflict.
  Claims chronically inadequate compensation (₹3,000 paid vs ~₹25,000
  market value for a cow killed by a tiger) has led to villagers poisoning
  cattle carcasses to retaliate against tigers/scavengers, and that STR
  forest watchers now run GPS-tracked routes specifically to find and
  neutralize poisoned carcasses before a mass die-off.
  **⚠️ Handle differently from everything else in this file.** This
  describes a specific, currently-ongoing illegal practice, potentially
  attributable to identifiable communities living near the reserve. Even if
  the citation checks out, this is not simply a fact to verify-and-publish
  the way an orchid record is — publishing it risks real harm (stigmatizing
  a community, tipping off where enforcement patrols already look, or
  being wrong in a way that damages trust with the exact communities this
  site says it won't misrepresent — see `people.html`'s own consent
  protocol). If this is ever considered for the site, it needs a judgment
  call beyond source-verification, ideally involving the NGO partners
  directly, not just a citation check.

**Explicit acknowledgment of vanishing practices:**
- Poongodi et al. (2011) state outright that ethnobotanical knowledge "is
  declining" and call for documentation before it's lost — directly
  supports the site's own "before it's gone" framing, and is the same study
  already cited above, so no new source needed to use this angle.
- Driskell's research also states Tamil Nadu's moratorium on FRA land
  rights has left Community Forest Rights (CFR) recognition at ~3% across
  the landscape, effectively freezing traditional land-stewardship
  practice. This lines up with the "25% of FRA title claims granted"
  statistic already on `people.html` (34,837 filed / 8,594 granted) — worth
  checking whether these two percentages (25% vs 3%) are measuring the same
  thing (individual titles vs. *community* forest rights are legally
  different categories under the FRA) before treating them as
  corroborating or conflicting.

## Prompt 5 — Geological uniqueness

**The Moyar–Bhavani Shear Zone.** Described as a genuine tectonic suture,
not just a visual meeting of hills: the Nilgiri Block (Western Ghats side)
and the Biligiri Rangan Block (Eastern Ghats side) structurally accreted
here millions of years ago. Sources cited generically as "Academia.edu" /
"ResearchGate" — **not a real citation as given**; these are hosting
platforms, not the paper itself. Before using this claim, the actual
paper/author/journal needs to be identified and pulled from whatever's
hosted there — same problem as an AI citing "a PDF on the internet" instead
of the work itself.

**Species shaped by the confluence:**
- ***Microhyla sholigari*** (Sholigari frog) — named after the Soliga
  tribe, said to be restricted specifically to the Biligirirangan +
  Sathyamangalam hill ranges (the overlap zone itself), not deep in either
  Ghats range alone. Source given only as "Wikipedia" — **needs a primary
  source** (the original species description paper) before this is treated
  as verified; Wikipedia is a starting point for finding one, not the
  citation itself.
- The recurring **tiger + blackbuck coexistence** claim resurfaces here,
  now attributed to "Tamilnadu Tourism" rather than the earlier "wildlife
  trackers." This is a *named* source this time, but it's a tourism board,
  not a scientific one — an improvement on "unnamed," but still not the
  standard the rest of this file holds itself to. Keep the existing flag:
  do not publish this claim until a scientific source is found, regardless
  of which non-scientific source keeps repeating it.

**Honest rarity check — this is the most self-critical part of any
response so far, worth preserving as-is rather than summarizing away:**
The response explicitly pushes back on "unique in the world" as tourism
marketing overstatement:
- **Within India**: the Vindhya and Satpura ranges also converge, at the
  Maikal Hills near Amarkantak — so STR is *not* the only place in India
  where major ranges meet. The more defensible claim is that it's the only
  such convergence specifically in **Peninsular India**.
- **Globally**: tectonic block convergences ("sutures"/"knots") are common;
  the Pamir Knot (Himalayas/Hindu Kush/Karakoram/Tien Shan) is the famous
  example.
- **The response's own verdict**: not geographically unique in the world,
  but a genuinely rare *tropical biodiversity land-bridge* — the real
  distinction is the tectonic collision forcing two different climatic
  biomes into one active wildlife corridor, not the mountain-meeting itself.

This is exactly the "flag uncertainty rather than round up" behavior the
original research prompts asked for — the "Peninsular India" framing is a
safer, still-genuinely-distinctive claim to use than "unique in the world,"
if the underlying geology paper checks out.

## Resolved — 15 August 2026: the core-area discrepancy

The site's long-standing open discrepancy (Forest Department 793.49 km²
core vs. Wikipedia/secondary-source 917.27 km²) is now settled. The
primary source this file's own "Next steps" checklist called for — the
2013 tiger reserve notification — was obtained directly from NTCA's
published records (`ntca.gov.in/assets/uploads/notification/tigerreserve/
Sathyamangalam.pdf`), a scanned PDF with no text layer, OCR'd locally with
`tesseract` after `pdftotext` returned nothing.

**G.O. (Ms.) No. 4, Environment and Forest (FR.V) Department, dated
16 March 2013**, declares Sathyamangalam Wildlife Sanctuary a Tiger
Reserve under Section 38(V) of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972,
effective 15 March 2013. It states: core area **79,349.331 ha
(793.49 km²)**, buffer area **61,491.240 ha (614.91 km²)**, total
**140,840.541 ha (1,408.405 km²)**. This confirms the Forest Department
figure and directly contradicts the 917.27 km² figure Wikipedia and
other secondary sources carried — that figure has been removed from
`data/atlas.db`'s `claim` table (was id 4, sourced to Wikipedia) and
replaced with a corroborating claim citing the primary G.O. (new
`source` id 55, new `claim` id 83). `exports/coverage.json`'s conflict
count dropped from 4 to 3 as a result. Site copy updated on `land.html`,
`govern.html`, `history.html`, `record.html`, `about.html`, `contact.html`
and `index.html` (the last three needed hardcoded "four disputed
figures"/"would settle" phrasing corrected, not just the core discrepancy
callout itself).

The same G.O. also confirms **no NBWL approval is referenced anywhere in
the 2013 notification** — the approval chain runs entirely through
NTCA/MoEF. This answers (in the negative) the open question from the
separate GTF/NBWL/WII/CBD research pass (15 August 2026, not yet written
up as a standalone file at time of this entry) about whether NBWL has a
citable role in STR's own founding notification: it does not, for this
specific document. NBWL does have a separate, real, STR-specific citation
elsewhere — a 2014 NBWL memorandum on road guidelines for protected areas,
cited in the Madras High Court's 06.04.2022 judgment (*Sp. Chockalingam vs
PCCF*, W.P. Nos. 1830, 4130 &amp; 4109 of 2022) upholding STR's night
traffic restriction — not yet shipped to the site as of this entry.
