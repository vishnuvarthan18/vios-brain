# Research & outreach plan — closing the coverage gap

## Why this exists

Vishnu's framing (15 Aug 2026): Sathyamangalam Tiger Reserve has comparable
or greater ecological/historical significance to places like Masinagudi,
but almost none of the regional attention — nothing is "properly
researched or properly maintained" the way coverage exists for Ooty/
Nilgiris/Masinagudi. The fix isn't inventing content to close that gap; it's
gathering real input directly from the people who hold it, and doing the
research properly, with them.

This is a companion to
[`heritage-research-findings-2026-08-14.md`](heritage-research-findings-2026-08-14.md),
which covers what's already been AI-researched and verification-checked.
This doc is the human-outreach side: who to actually talk to, and what to
ask each of them for, so future research prompts and site content are
grounded in primary input, not another AI pass.

The site's own `coverage.json` already quantifies the gap honestly (see the
new home-page "Why This Site Exists" section, shipped this session):
`community` domain at 0/100 (0%), `historical` at 1/600 (0.2%). Both of
those domains are exactly what outreach — not more automated research —
is positioned to fill.

## Four groups to work with

### 1. NGO partners (already in the picture)
The existing collaboration that originally flagged the Ooty/Masinagudi
coverage gap. Continue that relationship as the anchor for this work — they
likely already know which of the other three groups below are reachable
and how, and can vouch for the site's intent when approaching communities
directly (see #4).

**Ask them for**: introductions to community contacts, guidance on consent
protocols for any community-sourced material (the site already has a
"no misrepresenting community identity" standard — see
`people.html` and the Poongodi et al. naming correction in the heritage
findings doc), and a read on which heritage claims they'd want verified or
featured first.

### 2. Tamil Nadu Forest Department / STR officials
Authoritative source for figures the site currently shows as disputed
(core area 793.49 vs 917.27 km², tiger/elephant/leopard count
discrepancies in `coverage.json`'s `conflicts` array) and for anything in
the `legal` domain (currently 2/120, 1.7%).

**Ask them for**: the primary source behind the 2022 vs. 2024–2026 dating
conflict on the NH-948 night traffic ban (flagged unresolved in the
heritage findings doc), access to the full Tiger Conservation Plan
2019–30 and management-plan documents that feed the `documents` domain,
and a contact for official STR portal / press materials.

### 3. Local researchers & academics
People behind the studies already surfaced in research but not yet
independently verified: Karanth & Ranganathan (2018, Thengumarahada
human-wildlife study), Poongodi et al. (2011, Hooralis tribe
ethnobotany), Driskell (2018, FRA/FRC implementation). Also worth
identifying: whoever holds institutional access to the Mackenzie
Manuscripts (GOML Chennai / British Library India Office Records) and the
Moyar–Bhavani Shear Zone geology paper (currently only traceable to
hosting platforms, not a named author/journal).

**Ask them for**: direct confirmation or correction of what's attributed
to their work in the heritage findings doc, and — where their research
continues — an invitation to feature it properly credited rather than
summarized secondhand.

### 4. Soliga / Irula communities directly
Highest-sensitivity group. The `community` domain (consented oral-history
and ethnobotany) is at literal zero — the single largest honest gap on the
site. This is also where the standing caution in project memory applies
most directly: the Iyer (2015) cattle-poisoning claim in the heritage
findings doc is explicitly held back pending a judgment call made *with*
communities, not just a citation check, and that same care should extend
to any new direct engagement.

**How to apply**: this outreach should go through NGO partners (#1) first,
not cold — both for practical access and because consent framing needs to
be right from the first conversation, not retrofitted after content is
gathered. Only proceed once a consent protocol is agreed, matching what
`people.html` already states about how the site handles community
material.

## Suggested order

1. NGO partners — reconnect, share this plan, get their read on sequencing
   and community-outreach guidance.
2. Forest Department — pursue in parallel; official-source questions don't
   depend on NGO input.
3. Academics — reach out once specific verification questions are ready
   (the heritage findings doc's open checklist is the source list).
4. Communities — last, and only via #1's introduction and an agreed
   consent protocol.

## What NOT to do

Don't let this outreach plan become a reason to hold back what's already
verified. The home-page Recognition band shipped this session uses only
facts that passed the two-pass verification already done — that content
didn't need to wait for any of the above. Outreach is for the harder
domains (community, historical, legal, the still-unverified heritage
items) that genuinely can't be closed by more AI research passes alone.
