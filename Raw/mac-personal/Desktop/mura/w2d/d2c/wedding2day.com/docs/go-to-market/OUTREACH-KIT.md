# Venue outreach kit

For signing kalyana mandapams onto wedding2day.com. Written for Tamil Nadu.

---

## The problem with the obvious pitch

Mandapam owners in Tamil Nadu are already listed on Mandap.com, VenueLook, WedMeGood,
Sulekha, JustDial, WeddingWire, Weddingz, and several smaller directories. JustDial
publicly shows some of them fielding 70–200 enquiries.

So "list with us and we'll send you leads" is the eleventh time they've heard it this
year. It gets you a polite no, or worse, a yes followed by silence when a real booking
request arrives.

**You need a different reason for them to care.** You have one, and no directory can
copy it: you run the wedding, not just the listing.

---

## What actually makes you different

You have an in-house team doing photography, decor, catering, makeup, mehendi — the
whole thing. That matters to a venue owner for reasons that have nothing to do with
lead volume:

**Outside vendors are a headache for them.** Unknown decorators drill into walls.
Caterers leave a mess. Photographers overrun the slot and eat into the next booking.
Every mandapam owner has stories. A customer arriving with a known, professional,
accountable service team is genuinely less risk than one arriving with strangers.

**You bring a complete customer, not a lead.** A family that books a hall through you
has already sorted decor and catering. Fewer moving parts, fewer disputes, less
back-and-forth for their office staff.

**You can share revenue.** If they refer a couple who books your services, they earn.
No directory offers that — directories take money from venues, they don't send it.

That last point flips the whole conversation. You're not asking for a favour, you're
offering one.

## Call script

Adapt, don't recite. The point is the shape of the conversation, not the words.

**Opening — say who you are and why you're calling, fast.**

> "Vanakkam, sir. My name is [name], I'm from wedding2day. We're a wedding services
> company here in [city] — we do decor, catering, photography, the full arrangement for
> weddings. I'm calling because we're putting together a small group of good mandapams
> we can recommend to our customers. Do you have two minutes?"

Note what this does: you're a wedding services company, not another website. That's a
peer calling, not a salesman.

**If yes — ask before you pitch.**

> "Can I ask, when families book with you, do they usually bring their own decorators
> and caterers, or do you arrange it?"

Let them talk. You will hear the frustration. Every owner has an opinion about outside
vendors. Whatever they say tells you which angle to use.

**The offer.**

> "Here's what we're doing. We have families coming to us for the full wedding —
> decor, food, photography. They always ask us where to hold it. Right now we're
> sending them wherever, but we want to recommend three or four mandapams we trust in
> [city].
>
> If we list you, when we send a family your way they've already sorted everything with
> us. You get the hall booking, we handle the rest, and your staff doesn't have to
> coordinate with strangers.
>
> It costs you nothing. And if you send a family to us for decor or catering, we pay you
> a referral on that."

**Close on something small.**

Do not ask them to sign up on a website. Ask for something that takes them no effort:

> "Can I come by this week and take some photos of the hall? Fifteen minutes, and I'll
> set up your page myself. You don't have to do anything."

Going in person does three things: you get usable photos you own, you confirm the venue
is real, and you meet the person who'll be answering booking requests.

## Objection handling

**"I'm already on Mandap.com / JustDial / WedMeGood."**

> "I know, most good halls are. Those are websites — they send you an enquiry and that's
> where it ends. We're a wedding company. When we send you a family, the decor and food
> are already arranged with us. It's a booking, not an enquiry."

**"What does it cost?"**

> "Nothing right now. We're building this up and we want good halls on it. If it works
> for both of us we'll talk about it later. For now I just want to be able to recommend
> you."

Don't invent a price to sound legitimate. Free is your advantage while you have no
traffic — use it.

**"How many bookings will you send me?"**

Be honest. Lying here destroys the relationship on the first booking that doesn't come.

> "I can't promise a number, we're just starting. What I can tell you is we're not
> sending you tyre-kickers — anyone we send has already committed to us for their
> wedding arrangements. It'll be small numbers to start, but they'll be real."

**"Send me the details on WhatsApp."**

This is a soft no. Push once, gently:

> "I'll send it now. But it's easier if I just come by — fifteen minutes and I'll set
> everything up for you. Are you there tomorrow morning?"

Then actually send it, and follow up in three days.

**"Who are you? Never heard of you."**

> "We're new, I won't pretend otherwise. We're a services team that's been doing
> weddings — decor, catering, photography. [Give a real example: a wedding you did, a
> family you served.] Come and see our work, or talk to [reference]."

Have one real, checkable reference ready before you start calling. A venue owner who
can't verify you won't risk their dates.

>

> - Families sent to you with their arrangements already handled by us
> - No outside vendors your staff has to manage
> - No listing fee, no commission
> - Referral paid to you when you send a family to us for services
>

>
> - Fifteen minutes to photograph the hall
> - Your rates and capacity

>
> **[Name] · [phone] · [email] · wedding2day.com**

The "answer within a day" line is deliberate. Set that expectation at the start, because
a venue that ignores requests is worse for you than no venue at all.

## Rules for whoever makes these calls

**Twenty calls before you judge anything.** Early conversations are for learning which
objection comes up most, not for closing.

**Go in person for anyone who shows interest.** Phone gets you a maybe; visiting gets
you photos, a real contact, and a commitment.

**Take your own photos.** You cannot use images from Mandap.com or the venue's other
listings — those belong to whoever shot them. A phone camera in decent daylight is fine.

**Log every call in the prospect list, including the nos.** A no in July is often a yes
in October when their season is slow. Record why they said no.

**Set the response expectation on day one.** Tell them plainly: if a family requests a
date and nobody replies for three days, the family goes elsewhere and you both lose.

**Aim for 15–20 signed venues before opening the marketplace publicly.** Below that,
search results look empty and customers don't come back.

## Timing

Tamil weddings cluster around muhurtham dates from the Tamil panchangam, and those
cluster in particular months. Two consequences:

- Approach venues during their **slow season**, when the owner has time to talk and is more receptive to something new
- Have the platform live and stocked **before** the next muhurtham season starts, because that's when families are searching

Get the actual muhurtham dates for the coming year from a current panchangam and work
backwards from them. Don't guess at this — families plan around these dates precisely,
and being live a month late means missing a whole season.
