## Drawing notes

- Every drawing is an original redraw from historical motifs and public descriptions. None is traced from a photo, statue, flag, logo, Wikipedia file or Commons file.
- People are symbolic figures (pose, clothing, palm leaf, stylus), or a crown and emblem for kings. No face is drawn.
- Pandya: the parasol above the twin fish follows the brief. Whether a parasol belongs to the Pandya emblem itself is **not stated on the page** (unverified).
- Book motifs (anklet, bowl, bow, vel) come from each title or its story, as given in the meaning lines above.
- Script letters are drawn from the project fonts (Semmozhi Tamil, Brahmi, Vatteluttu, Grantha; SIL OFL 1.1) by `design/svg_kit/ttf_outline.py`.

### Batch 2 notes

- Temples and sites are **schematic** line drawings of a tower, a corridor, a tank, a trench: they show the TYPE of building, not the measured proportions of the named monument, and none is traced from a photo, plan or model. A person who knows the buildings should say whether each schematic is fair, or whether a drawing should be dropped.
- Every place date is either taken from the source named in its row or left **unverified**: all 11 site rows and 5 of the 15 temple rows carry no period on the page.
- Cultural icons are drawn as objects (lamp, conch, drum, staff, mould, frame). Nataraja and the vel are drawn plainly and neutrally; the Nataraja drawing is an original figure inside a ring of flame, not a redraw of any bronze or statue.
- The yazh has no surviving instrument: the shape is a reconstruction from the poems and later descriptions and is marked unverified.
- "State tree / flower / animal / bird" (palmyra, glory lily, Nilgiri tahr, emerald dove) is a claim about a modern government designation. It is written in the meaning line with its Wikipedia source and **nothing is verified**; a person should confirm each one.
- No government emblem, flag, party symbol, brand or living person is drawn. No Indus seal is drawn (owner decision).

### Batch 3 notes

- Trade goods, the month wheel and the festival items are **embossed on copper**; the daily-life items are **inked on palm leaf**. The 7 extra items added under item 13 of the prompt join an existing group and keep that group's material (culture copper, nature leaf, script stone).
- **Every batch-3 period is unverified**: all 25 rows carry `unverified` in the period column, so the page shows no date for any of them. The dates of the Roman trade, of the pepper, muslin, ivory and teak exports, of the pearl fishery, and the antiquity claimed for Pongal, Karthigai, Puthandu and the folk arts all need a source before any date is shown.
- The Roman coin is drawn **blank inside its wreath**: no ruler's head and no legend, because no portrait of a real person is drawn anywhere in this kit. A real Roman coin carries an imperial portrait; this drawing deliberately does not.
- The month wheel carries **no text**, because twelve Tamil words set around a 512-unit ring are unreadable at card size, and the twelve names are **not on the page either**: a 14 px English meaning line is below the 18 px minimum this project sets for Tamil text, so they are listed here instead and need a Tamil speaker to check every spelling and the order — சித்திரை, வைகாசி, ஆனி, ஆடி, ஆவணி, புரட்டாசி, ஐப்பசி, கார்த்திகை, மார்கழி, தை, மாசி, பங்குனி. The rule that fixes the start of each month is not stated anywhere and is unverified.
- Ivory is included as a **historical** trade good and the row says the trade in ivory is banned today.
- The ship is schematic: the rig and hull of Tamil ships of this period are not settled, and nothing is traced from an excavated hull, a model or a photo. The same holds for the bullock cart, the grinder and the fan: they are drawn as the type of object.
- Therukoothu and karagattam are drawn as the **headdress and the pot**, with a symbolic figure and no face; mayilattam is drawn as the peacock frame with no dancer inside it.
- The neem row names a home medicinal use with a Wikipedia source and **no health claim is made on the page**; a person should confirm or drop it.
- The Tamil numerals are set as real Tamil text from the project font (not outlined paths, which would break the 8 KB limit); a reader whose system font lacks ௰ ௱ ௲ will see the fallback font.
