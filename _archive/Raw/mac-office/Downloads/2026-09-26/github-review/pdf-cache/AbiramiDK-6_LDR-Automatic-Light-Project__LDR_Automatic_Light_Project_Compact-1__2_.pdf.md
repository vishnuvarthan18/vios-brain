          LDR Sensor Based Automatic Light
                       Circuit
1. Aim
To design and build a simple automatic light control circuit using an LDR, LED, resistor,
switching component, and battery, allowing the LED to respond automatically to changes in
surrounding light intensity.


2. Problem Statement
In many basic electronic applications, it is necessary to detect changes in surrounding light
and control a light automatically. A manual switch cannot respond automatically to changing
light conditions.

To solve this problem, a Light Dependent Resistor (LDR) is used as a light sensor. Its
resistance changes according to the amount of light falling on it, allowing the circuit to
automatically control the LED.


3. Proposed Solution
The proposed system uses an LDR and resistor as a voltage-divider circuit to detect ambient
light intensity. The change in voltage is applied to a switching stage that controls the LED.

In bright light, the LDR resistance decreases, while in darkness, its resistance increases.
Depending on the circuit arrangement, the LED can be switched ON in darkness and OFF in
bright light, or vice versa.

5. Circuit Working
The LDR and a resistor are connected to form a voltage-divider circuit. The voltage at the
divider changes according to the surrounding light intensity.

This voltage is applied to a switching stage. The switching component controls the LED
based on the detected light level.

When the light intensity changes, the LDR resistance changes, which changes the divider
voltage and consequently the LED output condition.

The exact ON/OFF behavior depends on the arrangement of the LDR, resistor, transistor, and
power connections.


6. Procedure
Connect the components to the breadboard according to the circuit arrangement.

Connect the LDR and resistor to form the sensing voltage-divider.

Connect the LED with the correct polarity.

Connect the switching component to control the LED.

Connect the battery to the circuit with correct polarity.

Check all connections carefully before powering the circuit.

Switch ON the battery supply.

Expose the LDR to bright light and observe the LED.

Cover the LDR or reduce the surrounding light and observe the change in LED condition.
