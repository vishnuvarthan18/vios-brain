W H Y D O W E E X I S T ? 	Three decades of civil war left a deep mark on Sri Lanka's socio-
economic and environmental ecosystems.
Wherever there is conflict in the global South, the international aid
organisations are there, and much of that work mattered. But the
imported solutions, from policies to products to programmes, were
not sustainably solving these fundamental challenges. Too often
they were designed without the people who knew the problem.
DreamSpace Academy was founded in Batticaloa on 30 September
2018, by Aravinth Panch and Kishoth Navaretnarajah.
W H A T D O W E B E L I E V E ? 	The people closest to a challenge are the ones best placed to
solve it.
Not because local knowledge is always enough; often it is not, and
the tools, capital and expertise a solution needs may come from
anywhere. But because a solution that is locally owned can be
adapted when conditions change, and defended when they
get hard.
What the people closest to a challenge most often lack is not the
insight. It is the capability: the skill, tools, networks, capital,
permission and standing that turn insight into a solution.

The mark is a honeycomb: interlocking
pointy-top hexagons. It is not drawn by eye –
the packing is exact.
For a cell of circumradius x, the horizontal
pitch is √3·x, the vertical pitch is 1.5·x, and
odd rows shift by half a horizontal pitch.
Every measurement in this book that
concerns the mark is expressed in x, so it
holds at any size.
The lock-up on its construction grid. One square = x, the circumradius of one hexagon cell – call it x.

In full colour the small solid cells are orange
and the outlined ones purple, and they
overlap. Colour is what tells them apart.
Flatten both to one ink and the overlaps fuse
– the mark stops reading as interlocking
cells, which is the whole idea of it.
The gap is cut, not painted. A painted gap
would show as a pale outline on every
ground but the one it was made for. Cutting
it leaves transparency, so one file works on
white, purple, orange, black or a
photograph.

The logo
is a honeycomb, so every
DreamSpace surface inherits that geometry.
It is what makes a page recognisable at a
glance without the logo on it.
For circumradius s: horizontal pitch is
sqrt(3·s, vertical pitch is 1.5·s, and odd rows
shift by half a horizontal pitch. Placing
hexagons by eye is guesswork; the packing
is exact maths, which is why every
DreamSpace comb is set out by
measurement, never by eye.
Cells sit at circumradius s − gap/2, with gap
≈ 6% of s. Touching cells read as one blob;
a wide gap stops reading as a comb at all.
Cell kinds
img 	A photograph, cropped to fill the cell
col 	A flat brand colour
split 	The cell halved on the vertical axis, two colours
txt 	A statistic: a big numeral over a caption, always in white ink
empty 	Left open – the comb must breathe, or it becomes a texture

DreamSpace's archive is thousands of
photographs of real people in a real town.
How they are used is a brand decision and
an ethical one, and the second governs the
first.
Dignity, not need. The subject is a person
doing something – building, teaching,
testing, deciding – not a beneficiary
receiving something. A photograph that
would embarrass its subject if they saw it on
a partner's wall does not run.
Consent travels with the file. A photograph
cleared for a workshop report is not thereby
cleared for a billboard.
Photograph
People working – hands on tools, at a bench, mid-build
The room as it is, including mess and half-finished
prototypes
Faces at eye level, from the subject's own height
Groups deciding together, not one person demonstrating to
a camera
The work itself – a machine, a circuit, a harvest, a repair
Do not photograph
Children isolated, sad, or shot from above
A person framed to look like they are receiving charity
Staged handovers of cheques, bags or certificates to
camera
Anyone unable to give consent, or a minor without a
guardian's consent
Documents, screens or badges carrying names, NICs or
