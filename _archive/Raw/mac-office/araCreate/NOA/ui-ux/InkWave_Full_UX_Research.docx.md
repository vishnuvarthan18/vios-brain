How to read this document
This is the complete UX research foundation for InkWave. It moves in one direction: from understanding the people, to understanding what they are trying to do, to the exact screens and flow we will build for them.

People: Sections 1–2 establish who we are building for and how confident we are in that picture.
Behaviour: Sections 3–5 go deep on what they are actually trying to achieve and where it breaks today.
Design: Sections 6–7 translate that understanding into structure and screens.
The flow: Sections 8–9 give the single end-to-end flow we follow, and what we must validate next.

1  Research Foundation


What this research is built on
Competitor evidence: Verbatim reviews from real B2B customers of UUNA TEK, published on their own product pages — including hotels, D2C brands, and real estate users.
Documentation: Official installation tutorials, product specs, and feature pages from Signascript and UUNA TEK, fetched and quoted directly.
Product scope: The InkWave product brief and four named target verticals: hospitality, luxury retail, real estate, e-commerce.
Design research: Established UX principles for non-technical B2B software (template-first design, role separation, progressive disclosure).

What we do not yet have
We have not yet interviewed real prospects, watched them attempt the task, or tested any InkWave screens with them. Everything here is a hypothesis to be confirmed. Treating it as fact would be a mistake — treating it as a strong starting point is correct.


2  Personas
Four primary buyer personas — one per vertical — plus one operator archetype that appears across all four. The buyer decides and configures. The operator runs the machine day to day.








3  Jobs To Be Done
Personas tell us who. Jobs-to-be-done tells us why — the deeper motivation people hire InkWave for. Every persona is hiring InkWave for the same core job, expressed differently.


Functional jobs — the practical task
Hospitality: When a VIP guest arrives, greet them personally, so they feel like more than a booking number.
Luxury retail: When a top client has a birthday, acknowledge them the way a friend would, so the relationship feels real.
Real estate: When farming a neighbourhood, make outreach that gets opened and read, so I get more callbacks than competitors.
E-commerce: When a customer orders, make their unboxing feel special, so they buy again and tell others.

Emotional jobs — how they want to feel
To feel proud of the experience their brand delivers.
To stop feeling guilty about cutting corners when things get busy.
To trust that the personal touch is happening consistently, even when they are not watching.

Social jobs — how they want to be seen
To be perceived as a premium, thoughtful, human brand in a digital, automated world.
To generate genuine word-of-mouth and social sharing from a memorable physical touch.

5  Journey Maps
Three journeys that decide whether InkWave succeeds. Each shows what the user does, what they are thinking, how they feel, and where InkWave must win. The emotion column is the signal — every dip is a moment we must design against.

Journey A  ·  First-time setup (make-or-break)
This is where competitors lose users. Camille has just received her InkWave machine.



Journey B  ·  Running a personalised campaign
The recurring core use. Sofia is sending birthday notes to 500 VIP clients.


Journey C  ·  Daily operation (the operator)
Priya configured a Shopify trigger weeks ago. A fulfilment-team member runs it daily.



6  Mental Models
The single biggest UX lever. Competitors expose the system's internal model (jobs, gcode, cells). InkWave must mirror the user's mental model instead. Every term in the interface follows the left column, never the right.

8  Core User Flows
Four screen-by-screen flows that cover the entire product. Each is built directly from the journey maps and mental models above.

Flow 1  ·  Onboarding to first letter
Goal: a real printed letter in hand within 10 minutes. Persona: Camille (first run).


Flow 2  ·  Create and send a personalised campaign
Goal: send personalised letters to a list. Persona: Sofia (500 birthday notes).


Flow 3  ·  Set up an automation (no code)
Goal: trigger letters automatically from another system. Persona: Priya (Shopify orders).


Flow 4  ·  Operator daily run
Goal: run today's queue with zero friction. Persona: the Operator.



9  The Final Flow We Follow
Everything above converges into one end-to-end path. This is the spine of the product — the journey every InkWave customer travels, from box to ongoing value.


Unbox → auto-connect machine → capture handwriting once → pick a template → preview → print first letter.
Success measure: the customer holds a real letter within 10 minutes, having never seen a driver, a cell reference, or a line of code.


Choose or build a template → add contacts by drag-and-drop → map columns visually → preview real recipients.
Success measure: a non-technical manager sets up a 500-recipient personalised campaign in under 5 minutes with no help.


Pick a trigger (Shopify / CSV / schedule) → connect in one click → set a condition → choose template + machine → turn on.
Success measure: letters fire automatically from a business system with no developer and no dedicated laptop.


Open Operator mode → load paper on prompt → press Start → handle any jam in plain language → done.
Success measure: an untrained staff member runs a batch in under 5 minutes without calling for help.


Every job logged → manager reviews history and volume → exports a report → justifies the investment.
Success measure: the buyer can show leadership exactly what ran, when, and for whom — something no competitor can do.
