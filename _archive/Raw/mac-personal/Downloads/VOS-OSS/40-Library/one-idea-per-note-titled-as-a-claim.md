# One idea per note, titled as a claim

A note titled "Pricing" is a folder pretending to be a note. A note titled
"Retainers beat project fees for small studios" is a thing you can agree or
disagree with, link to, and be wrong about in public.

The claim-title does three jobs at once: it forces you to actually finish the
thought, it makes the note findable by anyone (including an agent) searching for
the idea rather than the topic, and it makes contradictions visible — two notes
with opposing titles are a conversation, two notes both called "Pricing" are a mess.

## Why it matters
This is the cheapest habit in the whole system and the one that most changes
whether the vault is useful in two years.

## Where it came from
Standard practice in evergreen-note writing (Andy Matuschak, Zettelkasten
tradition); confirmed by long-running vault owners who report that *naming*,
not folders or tags, carried their organisation.
