India

All of India's nature and culture data,

Prepared by Vishnu · September 2026
  1     Where It All Started
 India is full of amazing nature. We have forests, national parks, tall mountains, big rivers,
 thousands of animals and plants, and hundreds of tribal communities with their own languages
 and ways of life.

 A lot of data already exists about all of this. The trouble is that it is very hard for a normal person
 to use:

  • It is scattered. Every piece sits on a different government or science website.
  • It is hard to read. Most of it comes as old files and long tables.
  • It is hard to trust. You often cannot tell where a number came from or how old it is.
 Imagine a student who just wants to know "Which parks in Kerala have the most forest?" Today
 they could spend days digging through websites and still not be sure of the answer.


  2     The Idea
 What if all of this lived in one place, and anyone could explore it on a map in a few clicks?

 That is India Data Atlas. We collect the data, check it, and show it in a way that feels simple and
 friendly, with the source shown next to every fact.


  3     What We Have Already Built
 The hard part behind the scenes is already done and running on its own:

  • It collects data automatically from trusted sources, every day, week or month.
  • It checks every fact before saving it. If a fact has no source, we do not keep it.
  • It keeps everything in one database linked to real places on the map.


   So the data is ready and waiting. What we need now is a beautiful website that lets people
   actually use it. That is where the UI/UX design comes in.



  4     What the Website Will Look Like
 The website has three main parts.

 A · Home Page
 This is the first thing people see, so it should feel welcoming and explain the project in seconds.

  • A short headline that says what the site is about.
  • Big numbers that grab attention: 602 parks, 6,196 peaks, 624 water bodies, 830 languages,
    585 districts.
  • Two clear buttons: Open Map and Explore Topics.
  • Topic cards, a small note on why people can trust the data, and a footer.

 B · Map Page
 This is the heart of the website. Everything we have shows up on one big map of India.

  7     A Promise to Local Communities

   Some places are special and need protecting, like tribal areas, sacred groves and traditional
   knowledge. We will never show their exact location. We only show them at district level. The
   design should make this clear and gentle, for example a softly shaded district with a small
   lock icon and a short note explaining why.



  8     How It Should Feel
  • The map is the star. Keep buttons and panels light so the map stands out.
  • Easy to find your way. The same menu everywhere: Home, Map, Topics and About. On mobile
    it folds into a menu icon.
  • Each topic has its own colour. The same colour shows on its card, its map layer and its page,
    so people always know where they are.
  • Everything is linked. From the map you can jump to a topic page, and from a topic page you
    can jump back to the map.
  • Works on every screen. Designed for desktop first. On a phone, the detail panel slides up from
    the bottom.
  • Friendly for everyone. Good contrast, works with a keyboard, and map colours that people
    with colour blindness can read.
  • Honest by default. Every fact carries a small source badge.
  • Nothing feels broken. Clear screens for loading, for "no data here yet", and for errors.


  9     What Comes Next
  • Park Health Score: one simple score for each park, worked out from satellite data on forest
    loss, fires and greenery.
  • Best spots for ecotourism: a map that suggests great places for nature trips.
  • More topics: laws and extinct species.


  10    What the Designer Will Deliver
  • User flows (Home → Map → Detail → Topic page)
  • Wireframes for the Home page, the Map page and the Topic page
  • Final designs for desktop and mobile
  • A set of reusable parts: menu bar, topic card, number card, layer switch, detail panel, source
    badge and legend
  • A clickable prototype of the main journey
