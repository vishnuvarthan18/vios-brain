# Niche Entry Routes into Indian Conservation, Forestry and Wildlife Research Technology: A Field-First Playbook for a CS Graduate in Erode

## TL;DR

The single most realistic path for you is to stop thinking "get hired" and start thinking "build a fundable pilot": pick one of three genuinely under-addressed technical gaps that sit on your doorstep (multi-reserve invasive-species time-series mapping of Senna/Lantana in the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve; drone thermal/LiDAR data-processing behind Tamil Nadu's 13-circle drone rollout; or an offline-first Tamil-language field tool for frontline staff), attach yourself to a Nilgiris/Anamalai host organisation (Keystone Foundation, NCF, ATREE, SACON), and fund it through a small grant that pays the host, not you.

Your B.Sc. is a real ceiling for exam and postgraduate-gated roles but NOT for the routes that matter here: conservation-tech NGOs (Technology for Wildlife, NCF) hire by interview and portfolio; the Ravi Sankaran small grant and Habitats Trust Action Grant explicitly fund non-postgraduate under-30 Indians; and MSME/DPIIT-startup registration unlocks government tech contracts your studio could not otherwise touch.

Move now on time-sensitive items: the Madras High Court Senna-removal deadline was 1 October 2025 (post-clearance regrowth verification is the live, unmet need right now); IGNOU Geoinformatics (PGCGI/PGDGI) July 2026 cycle closes 31 July 2026; Mohamed bin Zayed fund windows close 15 October 2026 and 31 January 2027; CEPF and other rolling calls are open. Build public GitHub/eBird/iNaturalist credibility in parallel because that, not another degree, is what gets you through the door.

## Key Findings

### The landscape on your doorstep is unusually favourable

You are sitting inside one of the most active conservation-technology policy environments in India. Erode district contains the Sathyamangalam Tiger Reserve and abuts the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve (NBR), Mudumalai, the Anamalai/Valparai landscape, IFGTB Coimbatore, SACON Anaikatty and TNAU's Forest College at Mettupalayam. Three things make this moment unusual: (1) a binding Madras High Court mandate and a first-of-its-kind state invasive-species policy are forcing large-scale, poorly-instrumented field operations; (2) Tamil Nadu has just deployed advanced drones across 13 forest circles but trained pilots only, not analysts; and (3) the state runs an SPV (Tamil Nadu Green Climate Company) that hires GIS people by direct contract rather than exam. Each is a wedge for someone with your exact skills.

### Gap 1: Multi-reserve invasive-species time-series mapping (your strongest opportunity)

This is the most tractable, most fundable, and most locally relevant gap for you.

What is happening: Senna spectabilis, an ornamental tree from tropical America, has, per The Hindu (2 November 2022), "taken over between 800 and 1,200 hectares of the buffer zones of the Mudumalai Tiger Reserve," spreading fastest in the Singara and Masinagudi forest ranges of the Sigur plateau. In The Hindu (1 April 2024), D. Venkatesh, Conservator of Forests and Field Director of MTR, put the spread at "over 1,500 hectares of the tiger reserve in the last few years alone," with roughly 400 hectares cleared since January 2024 and removed by TNPL for paper pulp. Lantana camara and wattle are the other dominant invasives across the NBR. The Madras High Court ordered removal from both Mudumalai and Sathyamangalam Tiger Reserves, and the Tamil Nadu Forest Department set a target to completely remove Senna from state forests by 1 October 2025. Per the Madras High Court bench of Justices N. Sathish Kumar and D. Bharatha Chakravarthy (reported via GKToday), "approximately 28,000 tonnes of this species remain in Tamil Nadu's forests," with TNPL and Seshasayee Papers and Boards Limited each to receive 14,000 tonnes; state proceedings record a conservation charge of Rs 350 per tonne. Under the TN PIPER Policy (Tamil Nadu Policy on Invasive Plants and Ecological Restoration, announced in the Assembly on 3 September 2021 and operational 2022-23), Lantana removal has run in all forest divisions since September 2024 at 50 hectares per month, rising to 75 hectares per month in severe protected areas from January 2025. By 28 February 2023, 2,969.29 hectares had been cleared statewide.

What is genuinely unsolved:

- No public, multi-reserve, time-series invasion map exists that stitches Mudumalai, Sathyamangalam, Bandipur, Nagarhole, Wayanad and BRT into one continuously-updated layer. MTR's own field director explicitly said landscape-wide coordinated removal is imperative because the species will reinvade from adjacent reserves, yet the mapping remains reserve-by-reserve and largely one-off.
- The existing academic work stops short of an operational tool. The citizen-science invasive atlas pilot in the Moyar-Bhavani landscape (Keystone Foundation and ATREE; contributors include N.R. Anoop, Shiny Mariam Rehel, R.S. Reshnu Raj, T. Ganesh) shortlisted 26 species for mapping and explicitly framed itself as a pilot to be scaled to the wider Western Ghats, central Indian highlands and Himalaya. A 2020 study applied Google Earth Engine to NBR land-cover but for degradation monitoring, not species-level invasion time series. A 2025 Biological Invasions paper used species-distribution models and literature review for management suggestions, and Anoop et al. (Biotropica, 2021) showed native mammals disperse Senna, but none of these is a live, post-clearance-aware map.
- Post-clearance regrowth verification is the live, unaddressed need right now. With clearance targets running to a court deadline, nobody is systematically verifying which cleared compartments are regrowing, which is exactly what a Sentinel-2 plus GEE change-detection pipeline is built for. Senna's bright yellow flowering canopy is a strong spectral and phenological signal.
- The invasive-biomass economy (Lantana briquetting units, Senna pulp to TNPL) has no public spatial or logistics tooling linking cleared-stock locations to processing units. The Tamil Nadu Forest Department and Supriya Sahu (Additional Chief Secretary, Environment, Climate Change and Forests) have publicly tied removal to tribal livelihoods (Kurumbas), which is a fundable social-plus-tech angle.

Buildable first project for one person: A Google Earth Engine plus Sentinel-2 time-series change-detection notebook that maps Senna/Lantana flowering-season signals across two or three adjoining reserves and flags post-clearance regrowth compartment by compartment, published as an open QGIS/GeoPandas workflow with a simple web map. This is squarely within your GIS stack (QGIS, GeoPandas, Rasterio, GEE) and needs no hardware. Pair pixel-based and object-based classification and document which performs better on Senna's canopy, because the literature genuinely has not settled this for this landscape.

### Gap 2: Drone thermal/LiDAR data-processing backlog (Tamil Nadu specific)

In late May 2025 the Tamil Nadu Forest Department, through the TBGPCCR project (Tamil Nadu Biodiversity Conservation and Greening Project for Climate Change Response; Chief Project Director I. Anwardeen), deployed drones with 48-megapixel cameras, thermal sensors and GPS across 13 territorial forest circles for elephant tracking, fire management and anti-poaching. Crucially, they are training only three staff per circle as pilots and plan a separate class of drones for terrain mapping, LiDAR/DEM, and forest-type analysis. There is no stated plan for who processes the imagery into orthomosaics, canopy models, thermal detections or LiDAR-derived products. Independent analysis (Mongabay India, August 2024) identifies data-processing time and technical know-how as principal barriers to ecological drone use in India, and notes that in Project Nilgiri Tahr thermal drones failed in daytime when rock temperature exceeded animal body temperature, a data-quality problem needing analytic correction. The TN Forest Department has a Geomatics Centre oriented to satellite imagery, not drone/LiDAR pipelines. (Note: an explicit official statement of a "backlog" was not found; it is strongly inferred from the pilot-training-only model plus the documented processing barrier.)

Buildable first project: An open, reproducible drone-data pipeline (photogrammetry to orthomosaic/DEM via OpenDroneMap, thermal-anomaly detection, and a LiDAR canopy-height workflow) demonstrated on any legally-obtained sample dataset, offered as a service to a forest circle or tiger-reserve foundation. This is your VTOL/thermal/LiDAR interest turned into a service the state visibly cannot yet perform in-house.

### Gap 3: Vernacular, offline-first tools for frontline staff

M-STrIPES (the NTCA/WII patrol and ecological monitoring system, launched 2010, mandatory across all tiger reserves) is Android-based and stores data offline for later upload where connectivity is absent, which is realistic. But its design assumptions skew to literate, English-comfortable operators, and reporting around Anamalai and Bandipur shows heavy reliance on training-of-trainers cascades. The genuine gap for Tamil, Kannada and Malayalam speakers and low-literacy forest watchers is a fully vernacular, icon-driven, voice-enabled, offline-first data-capture layer, and there is no widely-adopted open tool filling it. This is pure software, needs no hardware, and directly serves frontline staff you can actually meet at Sathyamangalam.

### Gap 4: Bioacoustics coverage gaps for Indian species

This is a live research frontier with a clear India-shaped hole. BirdNET was trained overwhelmingly on North American and European species: Kahl et al. (2021, Ecological Informatics) describe a deep neural network "capable of identifying 984 North American and European bird species by sound," and a 2025 global assessment (Ecological Indicators) confirms Africa and Asia perform consistently worse. The BirdCLEF 2024 Kaggle competition was built specifically around under-studied Western Ghats birds using data linked to V.V. Robin's lab at IISER Tirupati, which confirms the gap is real and actively worked but far from solved, especially with limited training data. Amphibians are worse off: as of recent literature there is no non-commercial multi-species frog classifier, despite frogs being ideal acoustic targets, and the first open frog classifier (VicFrogNET) is Australian. SACON Anaikatty (on your doorstep) has active bioacoustics work (for example Divyapriya and Pramod on Anaikatty Hills soundscapes; Shirish Manchi's cave-swiftlet acoustics). Chainsaw and gunshot acoustic detection, proven abroad by Rainforest Connection and by open AudioMoth-plus-CNN work in Belize, appears to have no published deployment inside Indian forests, which is a genuinely open niche (the closest Indian analogue is Indian Railways' fibre-optic Distributed Acoustic Sensing for elephant-train collision prevention, a different use case).

Buildable first project: A Western Ghats frog-call or Eastern Ghats bird classifier trained on your own AudioMoth recordings plus open Xeno-canto data, released open-source with a WILDLABS write-up. Or a low-cost chainsaw-detection proof-of-concept. Both are within edge-AI/CV skills you already have.

### Gap 5: Camera-trap AI beyond tigers

India's official pipeline, CaTRAT (Camera Trap Data Repository and Analysis Tool, WII, used in All India Tiger Estimation), and international tools like MegaDetector are strong on detection and on tigers/leopards, but species-level classification for India's smaller and cryptic fauna (small cats such as rusty-spotted cat, pangolin, civets, mouse deer) is weak, and camera surveys keep turning up first-reserve records almost by accident during tiger counts (for example rusty-spotted cat in Veerangana Durgavati in 2024-2026). The gap is an India-specific small-species classifier fine-tuned on Indian field conditions. Edge-AI deployment (TrailGuard AI by RESOLVE/Nightjar, in Kanha-Pench and Dudhwa since 2022) shows the appetite for on-device inference.

### Gap 6: Human-elephant conflict prediction versus detection

The Coimbatore-Sathyamangalam-Valparai corridor is the most instrumented conflict landscape in India, which cuts both ways. NCF's Valparai model (Ananda Kumar, Ganesh Raghunathan, Sreedhar Vijayakrishnan) built an Elephant Information Network from 2011: bulk SMS in Tamil and English to up to 1,500 people, missed-call-activated red beacon lights, and local-TV crawls. Per AFP/Phys.org (November 2014), NCF researcher Ganesh Raghunathan said research found "36 of the 41 deaths from elephant attacks in Valparai since 1994 could have been prevented if the victim had received a warning," and "up to 1,500 people, mostly tea pickers, are informed in English and Tamil." The Tamil Nadu Forest Department has since deployed AI-enabled thermal/IR camera command centres (for example 46 cameras at Nadugani, Gudalur, DFO N. Vengatesh Prabhu, camera points chosen from 10 years of detection data). So detection is crowded. What remains genuinely unsolved is prediction: forecasting where a herd will be tomorrow from movement history, crop calendars, water and moon phase, rather than reacting once animals are seen. That is a data-science problem suited to you, best done with NCF or the forest department, not alone.

### Gap 7: Compartment-level forest-fire prediction

FSI provides near-real-time MODIS/VIIRS fire detection and a weekly, zone-level Fire Weather Index danger rating on the Van Agni geoportal. It does not provide compartment-level, locally-parameterised daily fire-risk prediction. Indian research is filling this in the Himalayas and central India (for example Singh and Jeganathan at BIT Mesra built a random-forest model over Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh, published in Advances in Space Research, 2024, and explicitly noted it could be extended to the Western Ghats), which leaves Tamil Nadu and the Western Ghats comparatively under-served, a defensible niche.

## Details: Organisations, People, Funding and Entry Mechanics

### Conservation-tech organisations that hire by interview and portfolio, not exam

- **Technology for Wildlife Foundation** (Goa), founder Shashank Srinivasan. This is your closest role model: a small conservation-tech NGO doing GIS, drone and underwater-robot work, with public GitHub repos (techforwildlife: qgis-drone_path-plugin, UAV mapping mission plugins, forest-clearance web maps). Their hiring is interview-plus-test based; a published staff account describes being hired as Conservation Geographer after an internship and a warm interview/test process, and the founder's screening question was "do you want to do GIS because you like GIS, or would you do conservation." Contributing to their open repos is a concrete, verifiable way in. Contact: contact@techforwildlife.com.
- **Nature Conservation Foundation** (Mysore), with the Valparai human-elephant programme (Ananda Kumar's group) and a high-altitude programme in Spiti/Ladakh. NCF hires research associates and project staff by application; the Valparai team is exactly where an elephant-movement prediction project would live.
- **Keystone Foundation** (Kotagiri, Nilgiris), the single most geographically convenient host for you. It runs 30-plus projects across the NBR including Sathyamangalam, co-led the Moyar-Bhavani invasive atlas, and openly welcomes interns, fellows and volunteers year round (kf@keystone-foundation.org, communications@keystone-foundation.org). A GIS/invasive-mapping pitch here is a natural fit and Keystone can act as your grant-holding host.
- **ATREE** (Bengaluru), CEPF Western Ghats Regional Implementation Team and co-author of the invasive atlas; **SACON** (Anaikatty, near Coimbatore) for bioacoustics and ornithology; **IISER Tirupati** (V.V. Robin's Sky Island lab) which explicitly lists opportunities for volunteers, interns, project assistants and independent researchers via the YETI mailing list, with intern pay stated. Also relevant: Wildlife Conservation Trust (partnered with Technology for Wildlife on a Ganges dolphin drone survey), WCS-India, Wildlife Trust of India, Foundation for Ecological Security, BNHS, Aaranyak, and Agumbe Rainforest Research Station.

It recruits directly, not through TNPSC, and its careers page explicitly lists a "GIS and Mapping" internship/young-professional stream (no open vacancy at time of research). - **WII Dehradun, SACON Anaikatty, ICFRE institutes** (IFGTB Coimbatore, FRI Dehradun, HFRI Shimla), FSI, NRSC/IIRS, and state remote-sensing application centres routinely hire Project Associates/JRFs on contract by walk-in interview against sponsored projects, where a B.Sc. plus demonstrable GIS/coding skills can qualify. These are contract, not permanent, but they are real field-adjacent entry points that bypass exams.

### Funding you can realistically win without a postgraduate degree

- **Ravi Sankaran Fellowship / Inlaks-Ravi Sankaran Small Grant** (Inlaks Shivdasani Foundation). The best-fit scheme for you: open to Indian nationals resident in India, requires only a Bachelor's degree in any subject, applicant normally below 30, and explicitly seeks bold, unconventional ideas. The Small Grant funds a short India-based project up to Rs 2 lakh per year (stipend plus project expenses). Contact gladys@inlaksfoundation.org; check the Inlaks site for the current cycle deadline.
- **The Habitats Trust Grants.** The THT Action Grant supports individuals and organisations doing on-ground work on lesser-known species/habitats; the Research Grant (new in 2025) and Seed Grant are also relevant, and 2025 recipients included individuals (for example Bryan Miranda mapping guitarfish, Sunil Harsana on striped hyenas). Note the Conservation Grant is organisation-only with heavy eligibility (3 to 5 years existence, Rs 30 lakh average annual spend, 12AB/CSR-1 certificates). The 2026 cycle applications were shown as closed at time of research, so watch for the next window.
- **Rufford Small Grants.** Up to GBP 7,000 (first grant), then GBP 8,000, GBP 12,000, GBP 18,000. Crucial mechanics: Rufford pays organisations, not individuals, so you apply through an Indian host NGO (Keystone, NCF, ATREE routinely hold these); it prioritises MSc/PhD or those who graduated within three years and does NOT fund BSc students, but non-postgraduates who meet the other criteria and work through a host have succeeded. Rolling deadlines, one application per 12 months. References must come from institutional emails.
- **Mohamed bin Zayed Species Conservation Fund.** Up to USD 25,000, open to individuals, single-species focus, in-situ field work including salaries/stipends. Three cycles a year; upcoming windows close 15 October 2026 and 31 January 2027. Highly competitive: the Fund's own Funding Review Process page states "there are usually between 1,500 to 1,800 applications per year with about 12% of applicants receiving grants" (a 2026 Fund blog notes only 8.5% funded in a record year). NCF's Valparai elephant work has previously been supported by this fund.
- **National Geographic Society grants** (now RFP-driven rather than open-call, historically Level I up to USD 20,000, Level II up to USD 100,000, funds individuals in conservation and technology); **IdeaWild** (small equipment grants, ideal for buying an AudioMoth, a camera trap or a laptop); **Conservation Leadership Programme, Whitley Fund, CEPF Western Ghats** (ATREE-administered, cepfwghats@atree.org, has open calls with 2026 deadlines), **WILDLABS Awards, Conservation X Labs prizes**.
- **Government scheme funding**: CAMPA (afforestation/restoration funds routed via state forest departments), National Mission on Himalayan Studies (Himalaya only, so relevant if you take the Spiti/Ladakh route with NCF), Green India Mission, MoEFCC Green Skill Development Programme, and DST/ANRF geospatial or AI mission funding. Most of these reach individuals only via an institutional host, which reinforces the host-organisation strategy.

### Non-obvious entry mechanics (prioritise these)

- **Open-source contribution as a credential.** Contribute to Technology for Wildlife's GitHub, MegaDetector/Microsoft AI for Earth, Wildlife Insights, Wildbook/Wild Me, BirdNET, Rainforest Connection and OpenForis. A merged pull request or a useful QGIS plugin is more persuasive to these employers than a certificate.
- **WILDLABS.net.** The world's largest conservation-tech community, with a careers board, discussion groups, and the WILDLABS Awards (2025 round: two grant tiers, USD 50,000 and USD 10,000, 15 projects, Arm engineering support, and explicitly open to independent conservation-tech developers of all skill levels). Join, post your Senna or bioacoustics project, and use it to find a host and collaborators.
- **Citizen science as credibility.** Systematic contribution to eBird India, iNaturalist India, SeasonWatch and Frog Watch builds a public, verifiable naturalist track record that partly offsets the lack of a biology degree and gets you known to the exact people who hire.
- **Volunteer routes** that put you physically inside protected areas: the All India Tiger Estimation phase-1 volunteer surveys, state forest-department census volunteering (Anamalai and other TN reserves use M-STrIPES-based counts staffed partly by volunteers), and NGO/research-station volunteer and internship schemes (Keystone, Agumbe, IISER Tirupati via YETI).
- **Consultancy/vendor route, with a critical caveat.** State forest tech is procured via tntenders.gov.in (needs a Class-3 Digital Signature Certificate), the Central Public Procurement Portal and GeM. Many state RFPs carry domain-specific turnover clauses (a SIPCOT GIS RFP required GIS-services turnover over three years; a representative forest-department RFP required Rs 50 lakh annual turnover for three years plus profit-making status) that exclude a new studio. The single biggest lever to get around this is to register your studio as an Udyam MSME and a DPIIT-recognised startup, which on GeM unlocks EMD exemption and waiver of turnover and prior-experience benchmarks, plus L1-plus-15-percent price matching (MSMEs accounted for roughly 47% of GeM order value in FY2025-26). Realistic near-term entry is GeM services plus subcontracting under a PMU or larger firm rather than winning a big RFP outright. No currently-open TN/Karnataka/Kerala forest tender specifically for camera-trap AI, drone processing or invasive mapping was found; monitor tntenders.gov.in (Environment and Forests), forests.tn.gov.in/tenders and GeM directly.

### Part-time and distance qualifications that raise your ceiling without full-time study

- **IGNOU Geoinformatics**, distance mode, any-graduate eligibility: PG Certificate (PGCGI, 20 credits, six months, about Rs 6,000, built with ISRO/DRDO/IIT/C-DAC experts), PG Diploma (PGDGI, roughly Rs 15,700), and MSc Geoinformatics (MSCGI). The July 2026 admission cycle was open with a last date of 31 July 2026 at time of research. These are cheap, credible for GIS hiring, and stackable while you keep your studio running.
- **IIRS (ISRO) Dehradun** outreach programmes and free MOOCs in remote sensing and GIS, plus formal PG courses, are the most respected non-degree geospatial credential in India and directly relevant to forest-department and NRSC-adjacent work.

## Recommendations

### Stage 1 (now to 3 months): Build visible credibility and pick your wedge.

- Start contributing to eBird India and iNaturalist immediately, and make at least one useful open-source contribution (a QGIS plugin fix, a MegaDetector or BirdNET issue, or a Technology for Wildlife repo).
- Build the Senna/Lantana GEE change-detection notebook as a public GitHub project with a short write-up posted to WILDLABS. This is your calling card and doubles as a grant pilot.
- Enrol in IGNOU PGCGI before 31 July 2026 (low cost, distance, raises your GIS eligibility ceiling while you keep working).
- Register the studio as Udyam MSME and apply for DPIIT startup recognition to unlock GeM procurement later. Benchmark that should change your plan: if within three months you cannot get a single named person at Keystone, NCF, SACON or Technology for Wildlife to reply substantively to your project pitch, widen your outreach and reconsider whether the bioacoustics or drone-processing wedge gets warmer responses than invasives.

### Stage 2 (3 to 9 months): Attach to a host and get funded.

- Approach Keystone Foundation (nearest, invasive-mapping fit) and NCF Valparai (elephant/data fit) as grant-holding hosts. Pitch the specific pilot, not a job.
- With a host secured, apply for the Ravi Sankaran Small Grant (Rs 2 lakh, fits your profile precisely) and/or a Rufford Small Grant through the host organisation, and an IdeaWild equipment grant for an AudioMoth or camera trap.
- Volunteer for the next All India Tiger Estimation phase-1 survey or a TN forest-department census to get physically inside Sathyamangalam and build field relationships. Benchmark: a funded pilot of any size, or a paid project-associate contract, within nine months means the strategy is working. If not, pivot toward the paid drone-data-processing service route, which monetises faster.

### Stage 3 (9 to 24 months): Convert the pilot into paid, field-based work.

- Turn the invasive-mapping or drone-processing pilot into a service offered to a forest circle or tiger-reserve foundation via GeM (using your MSME/startup status) or as a subcontractor under a PMU.
- Scale a successful small grant into a Mohamed bin Zayed (up to USD 25,000), National Geographic, or Habitats Trust Action Grant application.
- If bioacoustics is where you gain traction, target a WILDLABS Award (USD 10,000 to 50,000) with a Western Ghats frog or Eastern Ghats bird classifier. Benchmark: by 24 months you should have either a recurring consultancy revenue line from state forest tech or a multi-year research-associate position at an NGO. If neither, the MSc Geoinformatics (IGNOU, distance) becomes worth completing to break through remaining eligibility ceilings.

## Caveats and reality check

- **Pay and permanence**: NGO research-associate and project-assistant roles in Indian conservation typically pay modestly and are contract-based, tied to project funding cycles, not permanent. TNGCC young-professional and consultant pay could not be verified from official sources (an unofficial aggregator suggested roughly Rs 30,000 to 50,000 a month; treat as unverified). Government project posts (JRF/Project Associate) are fixed-term. The field-based, non-desk life you want is real in these roles but usually comes with income insecurity for the first few years.
- **Degree ceilings you will hit**: permanent government scientific posts, ACF/Range Officer, and most WII/SACON academic tracks require postgraduate qualifications and often exams, which you have ruled out. Rufford will not fund you as a BSc-only individual (only through a host), and the Habitats Trust Conservation Grant is organisation-only. The routes above are chosen precisely because they bypass these ceilings, but they do not eliminate them for career progression, which is why a distance PG qualification is a sensible parallel bet.
- **Competition**: WILDLABS, National Geographic, Mohamed bin Zayed and Rufford are internationally competitive (Mohamed bin Zayed alone gets 1,500 to 1,800 applications a year, funding roughly 8.5 to 12 percent). Your edge is hyper-local specificity (Sathyamangalam/Nilgiris) plus real CS skills, so keep proposals concrete and place-based.
- **Geographic constraint is actually your advantage**: almost every gap identified sits within a few hours of Chithode. Do not dilute this by chasing Himalayan or generic-India projects early; your credibility comes from knowing the Moyar-Bhavani/Sathyamangalam landscape better than any outside applicant.
- **Time-sensitive and possibly out-of-date items to verify before acting**: the Madras High Court 1 October 2025 Senna deadline and the exact tonnage/allocation figures were current as of 2024-2025 reporting and the clearance status will have moved (this strengthens, not weakens, the post-clearance verification opportunity). IGNOU (31 July 2026), Mohamed bin Zayed (15 October 2026, 31 January 2027) and CEPF (2026 rolling calls) dates should be reconfirmed on the official sites. WILDLABS Awards ran an early-2025 cycle; confirm whether a 2026 cycle is open. TNGCC vacancies were nil at time of research; monitor the careers page. No currently-open TN/Karnataka/Kerala forest tender specifically for camera-trap AI, drone processing or invasive mapping was confirmed, nor an explicit official statement of a drone data-processing backlog (it is strongly inferred, not stated). No published chainsaw/gunshot acoustic deployment was found inside Indian forests, which supports the niche claim but is not absolute proof none exists.
