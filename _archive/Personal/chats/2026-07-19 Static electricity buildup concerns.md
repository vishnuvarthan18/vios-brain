Static shock buildup usually comes down to a few factors:

- **Low humidity** — dry air (common in AC-heavy environments or seasonal shifts) is the biggest driver. Skin and air both lose conductivity.
- **Synthetic fabrics** — polyester, nylon blends generate more charge than cotton/wool.
- **Rubber-soled shoes on carpet/synthetic flooring** — insulates you from ground discharge, so charge builds instead of dissipating.
- **Dehydration or dry skin** — less moisture on skin = less conductivity = more static retention.

Quick fixes: humidifier (40-50% indoor humidity), moisturize hands/skin, switch to cotton clothing, touch metal (not another person) to discharge before touching sensitive electronics.

If it's sudden and pronounced with no environmental change, worth checking hydration levels — that's the one internal factor that shifts this.
