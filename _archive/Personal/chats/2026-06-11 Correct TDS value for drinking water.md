# Correct TDS value for drinking water

## Summary
**Conversation Overview**

The person asked about correct TDS (Total Dissolved Solids) values for drinking water. Claude provided information referencing WHO and BIS (IS 10500) standards, covering a range of TDS classifications from excellent to unacceptable. The person then asked for a more direct, matching-value format, and Claude refined the response to highlight the practical sweet spot of 50–150 ppm, with 80–150 ppm cited as the most commonly recommended range by water purifier manufacturers and health bodies. Key points covered included that values below 50 ppm are too low and may require remineralization, while values above 500 ppm are not recommended for regular drinking. The BIS acceptable limit of 500 ppm was noted as a reference standard.

## Chat

**Vishnu:** What is the correct tds value for drinking

**Claude:** TDS for drinking water is measured in mg/L (ppm). Per WHO and BIS standards:

- **< 300 mg/L** — Excellent
- **300–600 mg/L** — Good (ideal target range for most people)
- **600–900 mg/L** — Fair / acceptable
- **900–1200 mg/L** — Poor but permissible
- **> 1200 mg/L** — Unacceptable (BIS max permissible limit is 2000 mg/L only where no alternative source exists)

**Practical target:** 50–300 mg/L. Below ~50 mg/L water tastes flat and lacks beneficial minerals (calcium, magnesium); RO systems often over-strip, so a remineralizer is worth adding if your output reads under 50.

BIS (IS 10500) acceptable limit is 500 mg/L; WHO considers up to 600 mg/L good.

**Vishnu:** Tell in tds matching value

**Claude:** Ideal drinking water TDS: **50–150 ppm**.

- **50–150 ppm** — Best for drinking (good taste, adequate minerals)
- **150–300 ppm** — Good, fully acceptable
- **300–500 ppm** — Acceptable (BIS limit is 500)
- **Below 50 ppm** — Too low, add remineralizer
- **Above 500 ppm** — Not recommended

Sweet spot most water purifier makers and health bodies cite: **80–150 ppm**.
