## Stage 1 — Research & Synthesis

**Method note (read first):** No interviews, analytics, or tickets exist. This is structured inference from the spec, the Calendar PRD, and domain reasoning. Everything below is a **hypothesis to validate**, not a finding. Confidence flagged where it matters. The job of this stage is to give Stages 2–3 a defensible foundation and surface the decisions that will bite later.

---

### Personas

Three, mapped to the three roles. The regular user is anchored to your Calendar PRD; the two ops roles are inferred from the Admin Portal spec.

**1. The Operator (`operator`) — "keeps the lights on"**
Platform-side ops/support engineer. Lives in dashboards. Reacts to alerts, investigates failures, triages user-reported issues. Today does this through terminal/SSH and raw logs. Has real power (can disable users, kill sessions) but is deliberately fenced off from destructive/governance actions. *Wants to see everything fast and act within their lane without fear of breaking something.* Read-heavy, write-narrow.

**2. The Admin (`admin`) — "owns the platform"**
Platform-side senior ops/engineering lead. Superset of operator. The only role that assigns roles, deletes users, and exports the audit log — i.e. the governance and irreversible actions. Accountable for who-did-what. *Wants control with a paper trail, and wants the dangerous actions to feel dangerous* (confirmation, friction, attribution).

**3. The Platform User (regular) — "just wants their merged calendar"**
From the Calendar PRD: a multi-account professional (2–4 Google accounts) who wants one view of total availability, privacy-preserving (busy/free, not event content). Already authenticated on the parent platform — araMetrics *is* that platform. Never sees, and never should sense, the Admin Portal. *Wants the app they were granted to just work, inside a shell that doesn't get in the way.*

The PRD's "privacy-conscious user" and "calendar admin/scheduler" aren't separate personas — they're **modes of the same platform user** (a privacy stance and a use case), so I'm folding them in rather than inflating the count. The "platform user — reuses parent identity" line in the PRD is not a persona at all; it's a **constraint** (SSO/shared auth), and it's the single most important integration fact for the shell. Treated as such below.

---

### Jobs To Be Done

**Operator**
- When an alert fires or a user reports a problem, help me find the cause across logs/errors/traffic *fast*, so I can confirm impact and respond.
- When a user account is misbehaving or compromised, let me disable it or kill its sessions *immediately* without needing an admin.
- When I'm investigating, let me see system and user state clearly without risk of changing anything I shouldn't.

**Admin**
- When access needs to change, let me assign/revoke roles and provision/deprovision users with confidence and a record.
- When something irreversible is warranted (delete a user, export audit), make the action deliberate and fully attributed.
- When an incident is reviewed later, give me an immutable, exportable account of what happened and who did it.

**Platform User**
- When I connect my Google accounts, merge them into one availability view *without exposing what my events are*.
- When I land in the platform, let me get to the app I actually use in one move, and trust that I'm seeing only what's mine.

---

### Domain map — what this platform actually is

A two-audience system sharing one shell:

- **Front of house:** lightweight, friendly, app-centric. The user's mental model is "my apps." Low cognitive load. (Calendar today; more later.)
- **Back of house:** dense, accountable, alert-driven. The operator/admin mental model is "the platform's state." High information density, desktop-first, governance-aware.

The hard design tension of v1: **one shell must serve both without feeling like two products bolted together.** The spec's answer — same shell, differentiated by role payload + a quiet admin context label — is correct and I'd keep it. The risk is the Home/dashboard, which has to be *two genuinely different surfaces* (personal summary vs. platform-health ops view) living under one route and one frame. That's where most of the Stage 2–3 effort will go.

---

### Pain points (current state → what we're replacing)

- **Ops by terminal:** no structure, no attribution, no guardrails. Anyone with shell access can do anything; nothing is recorded in a reviewable way. The Admin Portal's entire reason to exist is to replace this with structure + accountability + a permission fence (admin vs operator).
- **No separation of safe vs. dangerous actions:** terminal treats `disable user` and `delete user` identically. The portal must make them feel different.
- **No single source of platform health:** state is scattered across log files and processes. Overview must consolidate.
- **For the user:** fragmented calendars across accounts, and the privacy cost of sharing a calendar to show availability. Calendar Merger solves both.

---

### Insights that should drive Stages 2–3

1. **The dashboard is two products under one roof.** Treat the role-split Home as the platform's defining design problem, not a styling variant. Personal-summary and ops-overview need separate IA, separate component sets, same shell chrome. *(High confidence — it's in the spec.)*

2. **The permission fence (admin vs operator) is a UI problem, not just a backend one.** Operators see the same screens as admins but with fewer affordances. Decide the pattern now: hide forbidden actions vs. show-but-disable-with-reason. Recommendation: **hide** governance actions entirely for operators (cleaner, less tempting), but **show-disabled-with-tooltip** for borderline ones, so the role boundary is legible without clutter. Lock this in Stage 3.

3. **"Invisible to regular users" is a security property, not a sidebar filter.** The Admin Portal must leave *no trace* — no route that 404s suspiciously, no flicker, no mention in search. This constrains the global search (must be permission-scoped at the index level) and the routing model. Flag for Stage 3 IA.

4. **The admin context label is a safety mechanism.** Its job is preventing an admin from doing platform actions thinking they're in their own app, or vice versa. Design it as a persistent, unmistakable mode signal — not decoration. *(Spec-mandated; just don't under-build it.)*

5. **Irreversibility needs a consistent friction pattern.** Delete user, export audit, revoke sessions — these recur across the portal. Define one reusable "dangerous action" pattern (confirmation + attribution + maybe re-auth) in the design system, Stage 4, so it's applied uniformly.

6. **Privacy is the Calendar's core feature, not a setting.** Busy/free-only merging must be visible and reassuring in the UI — the user should *see* that event content is hidden, not just trust it. Carries into Calendar screen design.

7. **Modularity is the business, so the shell→app contract must be defined before any app.** What every app gets from the shell (identity, nav slot, search hook, context label, granted-state) must be specified as a pattern in Stage 3, or the second app (Calendar) won't "prove modularity" cleanly. This is the spec's central claim and the easiest thing to get wrong.

---

### Open questions to resolve before/within Stage 2

These don't block Stage 1 but will block clean IA. Flagging now:

- **Search scope:** does global search span *inside* apps (e.g. calendar events) or only platform-level objects (apps, settings, admin entities)? Affects whether search is a shell feature or a federated one. *My lean: shell-level objects + per-app contribution, permission-scoped.*
- **Provisioning entry point:** in v1, the super-admin grants Calendar to regular users via Admin Portal → Users. Confirm that's the only grant path (no self-serve), since it shapes the Users screens.
- **Operator's missing exit:** operators can disable but not delete users. Confirm there's no scenario where an operator needs to escalate to an admin *in-flow* — if there is, we need a hand-off pattern.
