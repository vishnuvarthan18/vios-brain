**Claude:** # IELTS Writing — Formulas & Band-Boosting Tips

Two tasks, 60 minutes total. **Spend 20 min on Task 1, 40 min on Task 2** (Task 2 is worth double). Write Task 2 first if you're a slow starter.

---

## Task 1 (Academic) — Describe a graph/chart/map/process

**Word count: 150+ (aim 170).** You *describe and compare data* — no opinions.

**4-paragraph formula:**

1. **Intro (1 sentence)** — paraphrase the question. Don't copy it.
   - *"The [chart/graph] illustrates [what] over [time period]."*
2. **Overview (2 sentences)** — the most important trends. **This paragraph decides your band most.** No numbers here — just the big picture.
   - *"Overall, [X] increased significantly while [Y] remained stable."*
3. **Body 1 (2–3 sentences)** — detail the first group of data *with numbers*.
4. **Body 2 (2–3 sentences)** — detail the rest, with comparisons.

**Trend vocabulary (memorise these):**
- Rise: increased, rose, climbed, surged, grew
- Fall: decreased, dropped, declined, plunged, fell
- Degree: sharply, significantly, gradually, slightly, steadily
- Grammar: *"rose sharply"* (verb+adverb) OR *"a sharp rise"* (adjective+noun) — vary both.

---

## Task 1 (General Training) — Letter

**Word count: 150+.** Match the tone to who you're writing to.

- Formal (unknown person): *Dear Sir or Madam* → *Yours faithfully*
- Semi/informal (friend): *Dear [Name]* → *Best wishes*

Cover all **3 bullet points** in the prompt. One paragraph each.

## Task 2 (both versions) — Essay ← **most important**

**Word count: 250+ (aim 270). 4 paragraphs.**

**Universal formula:**

1. **Introduction (2 sentences)**
   - Sentence 1: paraphrase the topic.
   - Sentence 2: state your position / what the essay covers.
2. **Body 1** — first main idea. **Point → Explain → Example.**
3. **Body 2** — second main idea. Same structure.
4. **Conclusion (1–2 sentences)** — restate your position. Add nothing new.

**Match the formula to the question type:**

| Question type | Position to take |
|---|---|
| Agree/Disagree | Pick ONE side clearly, defend it |
| Discuss both views + your opinion | Body 1 = view A, Body 2 = view B, state your side |
| Advantages/Disadvantages | Body 1 = advantages, Body 2 = disadvantages |
| Problem/Solution | Body 1 = problems, Body 2 = solutions |

---

## The 4 things examiners actually score (25% each)

1. **Task Response** — answer *every* part of the question, take a clear position, stay on topic. Off-topic = capped at Band 5.
2. **Coherence & Cohesion** — clear paragraphs + linking words (*Firstly, However, In addition, As a result, In conclusion*). Don't overuse them.
3. **Lexical Resource** — topic-specific vocabulary, some less common words, no repetition. One good synonym beats three fancy wrong ones.
4. **Grammar** — mix simple and complex sentences. Use conditionals and relative clauses. Accuracy > complexity.

---

## Band-boosting tips (highest impact first)

- **Hit the word count.** Under 150/250 = automatic penalty, every time. Learn what your handwriting count looks like.
- **The overview (Task 1) and clear position (Task 2) are the fastest band-jumpers.** Never skip them.
- **Plan for 5 minutes before writing.** Jot 2 ideas per body paragraph. Unplanned essays wander and lose Coherence marks.
- **Vary sentence structure.** All-short sentences cap Grammar at 6. Aim for a 50/50 mix of simple and complex.
- **Don't memorise whole essays** — examiners spot templates and penalise them. Memorise *structures and linkers*, not content.
- **Leave 3–4 minutes to check** spelling, articles (a/an/the), and subject-verb agreement. These are cheap points to lose.
- **Legible handwriting** (paper test) — if they can't read it, they can't mark it.

**Claude:** Total marks work differently from what most students expect. IELTS does not give you a mark out of 100. It gives a **band score from 0 to 9**, and Writing is scored on its own band, then combined with the other three sections.

Here is the full exam model.

The test has four sections. You take Listening, Reading, and Writing on the same day, one after the other, with no break in between. Speaking is a separate face to face interview, either the same day or within a few days.

Listening runs for about 30 minutes with 40 questions. Reading runs for 60 minutes with 40 questions. Writing runs for 60 minutes with 2 tasks. Speaking runs for 11 to 14 minutes as a conversation with an examiner.

Now to your real question about how much Writing counts.

Each of the four sections gets its own band from 0 to 9. Your final Overall Band is the average of these four bands, rounded to the nearest half. So Writing is one of four equal parts. It carries one quarter of your overall score, the same weight as Listening, Reading, and Speaking.

Inside Writing itself, the two tasks are not equal. Task 2 carries twice the weight of Task 1. So when your Writing band is calculated, imagine Task 1 counts once and Task 2 counts twice, and they are combined into one Writing band. This is exactly why I keep telling you to give 40 minutes to Task 2 and only 20 minutes to Task 1.

Let me show you with a simple example so it is clear.

Suppose you score Listening 6.5, Reading 6.0, Writing 6.0, and Speaking 7.0. Add them, 6.5 plus 6.0 plus 6.0 plus 7.0 equals 25.5. Divide by 4, you get 6.375. Rounded, your Overall Band is 6.5.

One important point for you. Writing is the section where most students score lowest, and a weak Writing band pulls your whole average down. If your target is an overall 6.5, you cannot afford a 5.5 in Writing, because it drags the other sections. This is why we are focusing here.
