# Does "Technology + Forest/Conservation" Have a Real Future?

**A deep research brief for Vishnuvarthan Venkatapathy**
**Compiled:** 26 August 2026
**Question:** whether technology applied to forests and conservation — geoinformatics, remote sensing, ecological data systems, biodiversity informatics — is a field with real, durable growth worth dedicating a working life to, in India and globally, as a builder, a researcher, or both.

**Method note.** Two evidence bases are used and kept distinct.
1. **New research**, conducted for this brief and cited inline with figures, years and sources.
2. **The Sathyamangalam atlas corpus** — 250+ projects individually verified across three research rounds, 25–26 August 2026, with lifespans, team sizes, funding models and failure dates recorded. That corpus was built to answer a different question, but it is the largest body of evidence available anywhere on what actually happens to small technology-for-conservation projects over 15–25 years, and it is used here rather than re-searched. Where a claim rests on it, it says so.

**Verification convention.** Every growth or trajectory claim carries a number, a year and a source. Where no solid evidence exists, this brief says so rather than softening it. **Three places where the evidence runs against the optimistic reading are flagged explicitly in Tasks 1, 3 and 5.** They are not buried.

# Task 1 — Is this a real, growing field?

## 1.1 The strongest single piece of evidence: a UN treaty target about biodiversity data

The Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework, adopted at CBD COP15 in December 2022 by 196 parties, contains 23 targets for 2030. **Target 21 is about data infrastructure.**

> **Target 21:** ensure "the best available data, information and knowledge, are accessible to decision makers," and strengthen "monitoring, research and knowledge management."

This matters more than any market-size projection. It means the thing this field does is not a service to conservation — it is a named obligation inside the framework that governs global conservation to 2030, with five-yearly national reporting attached.

The finance targets in the same framework:

| Target | Commitment | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| **Target 19** | Mobilise **at least US$200 billion per year** for national biodiversity strategies, from all sources | 2030 |
| **Target 19** | **At least US$20 billion per year** in international flows, developed → developing countries | **2025** |
| **Target 19** | Rising to **at least US$30 billion per year** | 2030 |
| **Target 18** | Reduce harmful subsidies by **at least US$500 billion per year** | 2030 |
| **Target 3** | **30%** of terrestrial, inland water, coastal and marine areas effectively conserved (baseline: 17% terrestrial, 10% marine) | 2030 |

Every one of those targets requires measurement to verify. Target 3 alone — moving from 17% to 30% of land under effective conservation — is a spatial data problem before it is anything else.

**Honest limit:** a treaty target is a commitment, not a disbursement. Whether the $20bn/year by 2025 was actually met is not established here and should not be assumed.

For someone in India entering this field now, that is a capability that did not exist when the current generation of practitioners trained.

## 1.5 ⚠ Where the evidence runs the other way

**This is the section the rest of this brief must be read against.**

### Conference activity in biodiversity informatics is flat to declining, not growing

From the Sathyamangalam corpus: *Biodiversity Information Science and Standards* (BISS) publishes peer-reviewed extended abstracts from TDWG and allied conferences — the field's principal technical venue.

| Collection | Year | Abstracts |
|---|---|---|
| TDWG Proceedings | 2017 | 153 |
| TDWG Proceedings | 2018 | 142 |
| SPNHC Proceedings | 2018 | 167 |
| **Biodiversity_Next** | **2019** | **368** |
| TDWG Proceedings | 2020 | **80** |
| TDWG Proceedings | 2021 | 125 |
| TDWG Proceedings | 2022 | 144 |
| TDWG Proceedings | 2023 | **159** |
| SPNHC-TDWG Joint Conference | 2024 | 142 |
| Living Data | **2025** | **100** |

**Read honestly: this is not a growth curve.** 2019's 368 was a one-off joint conference. Excluding it, the series runs 153 → 142 → 80 → 125 → 144 → **159 (peak, 2023)** → 142 → **100 (2025)**. The most recent year is the second-lowest in the series, and 35% below the 2023 peak.

Possible explanations — conference format changes, a shift to other venues, post-pandemic travel economics — are not established here. **What is established is that the technical-conference indicator does not support a growth story, and anyone claiming this field is booming should be asked to account for it.**

### Regional conservation funding can close, and the one covering the Western Ghats has

| Fund | Amount | Period | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CEPF Western Ghats & Sri Lanka hotspot** | **US$6 million** | **2008–2015** | **CLOSED — "previously invested"** |

Implemented regionally through ATREE, and focused exclusively on the Western Ghats portion. This is the fund that financed the **Western Ghats Portal** from November 2010 (Sathyamangalam corpus, Round 2). That portal now holds "over 10,000 observations" for a 129,037 km², six-state landscape and has been absorbed into the India Biodiversity Portal.

**The causal chain is visible and instructive:** a regional fund opened, financed a landscape portal, closed after seven years, and the portal plateaued. Round 1 of the atlas corpus found this pattern across the whole global sample — grant-funded builds dying at years 4–6, on schedule — and here it is in the user's own landscape.

## 1.6 Long-range forecasts: thin

**A credible, specific, 10+ year forecast from a government, UN body or major research institution on the role of data and technology in conservation and forestry could not be located for this brief.**

What exists instead: the GBF's 2030 targets (eight years out at adoption, four now), and India's biennial ISFR cycle. Both are commitments and programmes, not forecasts.

**This is a genuine gap in the evidence, and it should be treated as one.** Anyone offering a "conservation tech will be worth $X billion by 2035" number should be asked for their methodology; nothing of that kind was found from a source of the quality this brief requires.

**The corpus's central quantitative finding on this:** solo and near-solo projects **outlive grant-funded academic projects by three to four times.** Survivors cluster at **15–24 years**; grant-funded builds die at **years 4–6**, on schedule.

The mechanism is not mysterious. A solo project has no funding cliff to fall off, so its only failure mode is the person stopping. A grant-funded project has a scheduled, predictable death built in from day one unless it acquires an institutional host before the money ends.

## 2.2 The honest counter-examples

These are the same corpus, same verification date. They are not failures of ambition; most produced work of real quality and then stopped.

| Project | Person | Froze at | What had been built |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Illinois Wildflowers** | **Dr John Hilty** | **© 2002–2020** | Habitat-organised species pages **plus** literature-derived databases of plant-feeding insects, flower-visiting insects and vertebrate–plant interactions. Corpus verdict: "the best US example of a one-person site of genuine scholarly quality" |
| **BONAP / North American Plant Atlas** | John T. Kartesz (widely reported; not verified from the site) | **2014 edition, dated 11/17/2014** | A continental county-level plant atlas — **still widely cited as current, twelve years after its last release** |
| **E-Flora BC / E-Fauna BC** | **Brian Klinkenberg**, coordinator | Blog last post **16 Sep 2021**; a 2020 post documents recovering from a mapping outage | The most ambitious volunteer-edited province-wide two-kingdom atlas in North America |
| **FaunaParaguay** | A single researcher | **Expired TLS certificate** at check, 25 Aug 2026 — browsers now warn visitors | Species accounts **plus a historical literature and bibliography archive** |
| **Alaska Native Place Names Project** | Gary Holton associated (unverified as current) | Most recent dated content **2016** | Stated aim was "an authoritative statewide database of Indigenous place names" — never publicly delivered |
| **PNGplants** | **Barry Conn** | Live but undated | **One person is the botanical web infrastructure for an entire country.** Its sibling `pngtrees.org` has already lost its domain |

**The pattern is specific and worth internalising.** Solo projects in this field do not decay gradually. They run at full quality for one to two decades and then stop cleanly, leaving a frozen but often still-valuable site. The corpus's diagnostic: *"blog silence is the leading indicator of atlas death."*

And a harsher observation from the same corpus: **BONAP has not shipped since 2014 and is still cited as current.** A frozen resource in this field can keep accruing authority long after its maintainer stopped — which is a reason to publish dates, and a warning about what the field does not notice.

## 2.3 Is there a recognised category of person who does this?

**Yes — and it acquired its institutional furniture within the last decade.**

| Evidence | Detail |
|---|---|
| A named community with a name for itself | **WILDLABS**, founded 2015, **12,000+ members across 135+ countries** by July 2025 |
| A recurring self-assessment | **State of Conservation Technology**, five annual survey rounds 2021–2026, **1,073 respondents across 101 countries** |
| Peer-reviewed literature about the field as a field | *A global community-sourced assessment of the state of conservation technology*, **Conservation Biology** |
| Dedicated funding instruments naming the role | **WILDLABS Awards** and **The Boring Fund** (see Task 3) |
| A technical standards body | **TDWG** / Biodiversity Information Standards, with its own journal (BISS) and 1,580 peer-reviewed abstracts 2017–2025 |
| Named job titles in the corpus | "Biodiversity Informatics Research Center" (CRBio, Costa Rica); "National Wildlife Database Cell" (WII, India) |

**The honest qualification:** "conservation technologist" is a recognisable **community identity** with funding attached. It is not yet a standardised **profession** with accreditation, a defined training path, or portable credentials. The corpus contains almost no examples of people whose job title is that phrase; it contains many people who are botanists, ecologists, programmers or librarians *doing* it.

For someone deciding whether to enter: **the field is coherent enough to belong to and too young to be gatekept.** That combination is unusual and it does not last forever.

## 3.2 ⚠ The honest reading of those numbers

**Three things this table says that a summary would hide.**

**First: the field is growing in people much faster than in money.** WILDLABS received **more than 1,250 applications in 2025** against a combined pot of roughly **US$430,000** (Awards $350,000 + Boring Fund $80,000). That is approximately **US$344 of available funding per application**. This is not an underserved field waiting for entrants. It is a crowded one.

**Second: most of the major international small-grant funders exclude India.** JRS is sub-Saharan Africa. GBIF BID is Africa, Latin America and the Caribbean, and the Pacific. OFB is France. NSF ADBC is the US. CEPF's Western Ghats window closed in 2015. **The instruments that do cover India are Rufford and the WILDLABS programmes** — and Rufford's ladder is designed to terminate.

**Third — and this is the finding that matters most:** across the entire 250+ project corpus, **not one of the fifteen-to-twenty-four-year solo survivors was sustained by conservation-technology grants.** Not Plantarium. Not atlas-roslin.pl. Not Flowers of India. Not Minnesota Wildflowers. Not vncreatures. Not WikiAves.

The corpus states it directly: *"Money is not what determines survival in this corpus."* INBio in Costa Rica had 3.7 million specimens and international funding, wound down in 2014–2016, and its portal and its successor portal are both effectively gone. `vncreatures.net` — one person, no institution, no visible funding — added entries on 17 August 2026.

**What that means for a life decision, stated plainly: the evidence strongly supports this field sustaining a body of work. It much more weakly supports it sustaining a livelihood.** The long survivors had other livelihoods. Zoë Devlin published books. Katy Chayka was a Master Naturalist volunteer. The corpus's own note on Nature of Dorset quotes its author directly: *"This is my personal guide… it is my hobby, not my livelihood."*

The corpus contains worked examples of what running this on near-zero budget actually looks like.

| Project | Infrastructure | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **NatureSpot** (Leicestershire & Rutland) | Commodity shared hosting (**Clook**); volunteer-run registered charity | 8,495 species; species-of-the-month posts through July 2026; ~15 years |
| **Biodiversity of Sri Lanka** | **Blogspot** | Live; representative of a large hidden layer of solo efforts on free platforms |
| **Catálogo de la Flora de Cuba** | **WordPress.com** | A national flora catalogue on the minimum viable platform |
| **SEOSAW**, **Biodiversidata** | **GitHub** | Open-by-construction; Biodiversidata built Uruguay's first consolidated national dataset this way |
| **Ohio Odonata Society** | **Collection delegated entirely to iNaturalist**; the society site is only the interpretive layer | The cheapest possible atlas architecture in the corpus |
| **`biodiversity-gap-audit`** | One person, **Python + Streamlit** | Audits **26 million GBIF occurrence records** against IUCN Red List criteria |

**What made it possible, in every case: not owning the expensive part.** Capture delegated to eBird or iNaturalist; hosting on commodity infrastructure or GitHub Pages; publication through a GBIF IPT rather than a bespoke portal; and — critically — **the work being someone's serious avocation rather than their income.**

The corpus's counter-finding is equally documented: **UkrBIN (Ukraine)** returned a raw database error, *"Error 1040: Too many connections."* Not abandoned — resource-starved. The corpus calls this "the most common realistic death of a small atlas, and it is invisible from the outside."

## 3.4 The one earned-revenue model — and why it does not yet exist in India

Round 3 of the atlas research established this with real prices.

**In the UK**, county records centres sell ecological desk studies to consultants working planning deadlines: **Gloucestershire charges £155 for a 1 km search, £175 for 2 km, £225 for 3 km, £295 for 5 km, five working days**, with a 25% volume discount. Buckinghamshire's start at **£150+VAT**, with **up to 100% discount for non-commercial users**. **Essex Field Club** runs this service off the same **7,593,231-record** database that powers its free public atlas.

**In India, this layer does not exist.** Round 3 searched for it specifically and confirmed the negative: NABET-accredited EIA consultancies sell "Ecology & Biodiversity Study" as a component of an EIA, computing Importance Value Index and Shannon-Wiener from **their own fieldwork**, citing IUCN, WCMC, ZSI and BSI as reference material. **None holds a records database. None publishes prices. There is no records-centre layer to buy from.**

**This is an opportunity with no incumbent and no proven willingness to pay.** Both halves of that sentence are load-bearing.

# Task 4 — The next 10–20 years

## 4.1 What is already committed, dated, and therefore near-certain

These are not forecasts. They are commitments already made with dates attached.

| Driver | Commitment | Date |
|---|---|---|

**The most reliable statement anyone can make about this field's next five years** is that 196 countries have committed to measuring something they currently cannot measure well, on a five-yearly reporting cycle, with a 2030 deadline. That is a measurement problem with a legal deadline.

## 4.2 The technology shift that has already happened

**Satellite data becoming cheaper and more available is not a forecast — it is a completed event.** NISAR launched 30 July 2025 with an open data policy and an Indian distribution path through Bhoonidhi. Bhuvan already offers Land Cover, Land Use, CartoDEM, IRS high-resolution data, thematic layers, historical flood mapping and hyperspectral (HySI) products with OGC services and clip-and-ship.

**AI applied to ecological monitoring is at an earlier stage than the discourse suggests.** The GBIF data-use analysis found machine-learning approaches to be "a rising trend" but "currently small numbers," with species distribution modelling still dominant. WILDLABS' five-year survey finds camera traps and GIS/remote sensing remain the **foundational** technologies, with acoustics and AI "increasing." **The honest read: AI in this field is real and growing but is not yet the centre of gravity.** Anyone entering should be competent in the foundational layer first.

## 4.3 The concrete India gap an independent person could be early to

The brief asked for something specific and evidenced, not "there is always a need." There is one, and it is unusually well documented.

### The gap: 272,648 statutory village biodiversity registers exist, ~4.4% are verified, and none are digitised

| Figure | Value | As of |
|---|---|---|
| Biodiversity Management Committees constituted | **276,653** | **August 2026** |
| **People's Biodiversity Registers prepared** | **272,648** | **August 2026** |
| PBRs **verified** | **11,951** (≈**4.4%**) | May 2023, not superseded |
| Digitisation status | **None** — "no digitalisation currently exists" | 2024 reporting |
| Legal basis | **Biological Diversity Act 2002**; BMCs under s.41; PBRs under Rule 22(6) of the Biological Diversity Rules 2004 | |
| Deadline set by the National Green Tribunal | **2020** — missed | |

Each register documents local flora, fauna, traditional knowledge of medicinal plants, local livelihoods and landscape, at village level. Growth since May 2023 has been roughly **5,040 registers in three years** — the programme is essentially complete in creation terms. **The gap is verification and digitisation, and it has been the gap for years.**

**Why this is the specific opportunity:**

1. It is **statutory**, so it will not be cancelled.
2. It is **already written** — this is a digitisation and reconciliation problem, not a fieldwork problem, which is exactly what a small technical team can do.
3. It is **village-indexed**, which makes it composable with LGD codes, census data and revenue records.
4. **Nobody is doing it.** Documented funding shortage; no programme found.
5. It sits at the intersection of the two things that are simultaneously true about this field: enormous existing paper records, and almost no digital infrastructure.

**The ethical weight is real and must be stated.** These registers were compiled from community knowledge. The Maduranthakam case is documented: medicinal-plant experts contributed knowledge of common nettle and residents received no compensation. Any work here is a benefit-sharing question before it is a technical one, and the corpus contains the right precedents — **Mukurtu CMS**, where communities hold cultural protocols and protocol stewards control access, and **Sarawak Biodiversity Centre**, which documents traditional knowledge under a legal access-and-benefit-sharing mandate and deliberately does not publish it.

### The second gap: nobody in India reconciles conflicting official figures

The atlas research documented **seven** demonstration cases, two of them national:

| Case | The conflict |
|---|---|
| Sathyamangalam reserve area | **Five** published figures: 1,408.40 / 1,411.60 / 1,408.6 / 1,408 / **1,455 km²** |
| Sathyamangalam tiger population | **Twelve** figures across five methods; 2010 alone gives **12 and 46**, both attributed to state surveys |
| **WII protected areas, national** | **1,014 vs 682** protected areas; **175,169 vs 164,074 km²** — on two pages of the same government institute's site |
| Elephant corridors, national | **88** (2010) → **101** (2017) → **150** (2023), with a published methodological critique |
| Western Ghats ESA | **129,037** (Gadgil 2011) vs **59,940** (Kasturirangan 2012) vs **56,825 km²** (draft, July 2026) — **unresolved, deadline extended to 2027** |
| Sacred groves | **13,270** documented vs **100,000–150,000** estimated |
| Tamil Nadu tanks | **~39,000** (peer-reviewed) vs **119,797** (commercial tool) |

**The corpus's conclusion after examining 250+ projects worldwide: no project anywhere presents a disputed figure as a first-class object with competing sources attached.** The component mechanisms exist — Wikidata's deprecated rank, TaiCOL's dual status fields, BioModelos' competing distribution hypotheses, the Galápagos checklist's `cryptogenic` category — but nobody has assembled them.

# Task 5 — Verdict

## 5.1 The verdict, stated directly

**Yes — with a distinction that matters more than the yes.**

The evidence supports treating technology applied to forests and conservation as a **field with real, durable, institutionally-anchored growth, worth dedicating a working life to.** It does **not** support the assumption that the field will pay for that life.

Those are two different claims and the evidence for them is of very different strength.

| Claim | Verdict | Confidence | What it rests on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The field is real and will still exist in 20 years** | **Yes** | **High** | A UN treaty target (GBF Target 21) explicitly about biodiversity data, binding on 196 parties to 2030 with five-yearly reporting; India's 37-year, 18-edition biennial remote-sensing forest assessment; NISAR operational with open data since July 2025; GBIF record growth tracked as a formal global biodiversity indicator |
| **An individual can build a lasting body of work in it, alone or in a tiny team** | **Yes** | **High** | Sixteen named solo and near-solo projects verified at 15–24 years, several updated within days of checking; the corpus finding that solo projects outlive grant-funded ones by 3–4× |
| **The field can be a recognised identity you belong to** | **Yes** | **Moderate-to-high** | WILDLABS at 12,000+ members across 135+ countries, growing 20% in seven months; five years of self-assessment surveys; peer-reviewed literature about the field as a field; dedicated funding instruments. **But no accreditation, no standard training path, no portable credential** |
| **The field will fund an independent person's living** | **No — or not yet** | **Low confidence in a yes; moderate-to-high confidence in the no** | ~US$344 of available WILDLABS funding per application in 2025; most major international funders exclude India; CEPF's Western Ghats window closed in 2015; and **not one** of the 15–24-year solo survivors in a 250+ project corpus was sustained by conservation-technology grants |
| **The field is booming right now** | **Not established** | — | The BISS/TDWG abstract series peaked in 2023 at 159 and fell to 100 in 2025. No credible 10+ year forecast from a government, UN or major research body could be located |

## 5.2 What this means, put plainly

The honest shape of this field is **not** a growing industry with room for entrants. It is **an under-built public infrastructure with a legal deadline attached and more willing hands than money.**

That is a genuinely good thing to give a life to. It is a bad thing to expect a salary from in the near term, and the evidence on that point is unusually clear rather than merely absent.

The people in the corpus who lasted twenty years did not solve the money problem. They **sidestepped it** — they had another livelihood and did this seriously alongside it, for decades, and the work outlasted most of the funded institutional projects around them. Zoë Devlin, Marek Snowarski, Katy Chayka, Jorge La Grotteria, the person behind `vncreatures.net`. Their work is now cited, used, and in several cases adopted as regional reference standards.

**That is a real and available shape for a life's work.** It is just not the shape of a career, and pretending otherwise would be the encouragement you asked me not to give.

There is a second shape in the corpus, and it is the one your circumstances point at: **Krushnamegh Kunte's.** A named scientist inside an institution, running a peer-reviewed, editorially-governed volunteer platform with 1,000+ participants. Institutional salary, independent body of work, editorial authority. That is neither pure independence nor pure employment, and it is the only path in the corpus that solves both problems at once. Your existing plan — technical roles at WII, FSI, ICFRE or NRSC, with the IGNOU M.Sc. Geoinformatics behind it — is a route toward exactly that shape, and this research strengthens rather than weakens it.

## 5.3 Where the evidence is thin, stated without softening

1. **No credible long-range forecast exists.** No government, UN body or major research institution publishes a 10+ year projection for data and technology in conservation that meets this brief's evidence standard. Anything you read claiming a market size for "conservation tech in 2035" should be treated as marketing until its method is shown.
2. **The conference indicator does not support a growth story.** BISS abstracts: 159 in 2023, 142 in 2024, 100 in 2025. The cause is not established. This is the single strongest piece of counter-evidence in this brief and it should not be explained away.
3. **India-specific spending on remote sensing for forestry is not published.** The Department of Space's "Space Applications" line is a proxy covering far more than forestry.
4. **Nobody publishes success rates** for Rufford, the Boring Fund or the WILDLABS Awards. The 1,250-applications-to-$430,000 ratio is inferred from two published figures, not a stated success rate.
5. **How the long-survivors actually paid their bills is undocumented** in every case but one. The inference that they had other livelihoods is strong but rests on absence of funding records plus a single direct statement.
6. **Whether GBF Target 19's US$20bn/year by 2025 was met is not established here.** If it was badly missed, the finance case in Task 1 weakens considerably.

## 5.4 The single most credible next move

Not a job application. Not another research round. **Publish one small, complete, citable thing, with a DOI, in the next twelve months.**

Specifically: **a Sathyamangalam place-name gazetteer dataset, in Linked Places Format, deposited with a DOI, accompanied by a short data paper.**

**Why this and not something more ambitious:**

- **It is the only thing in the whole research corpus that is simultaneously: bounded, achievable solo, has no existing competitor, and is directly usable by others.** The Tamil Nadu District Gazetteers exist and are not available as structured data. Nobody has done it.
- **It converts you from a person with a plan into a person with a citable artefact.** The corpus's credibility ladder puts a peer-reviewed description of your own resource on the bottom rung and it is the only rung executable without anyone's permission — no institution, no funding, no gatekeeper.
- **It is the correct opening move for every downstream path.** For a Rufford application it is proof of work. For a WII or FSI conversation it is a demonstrated capability. For the French Institute of Pondicherry — whose Muthusankar Gowrappan leads both the Historical Atlas of South India and the Western Ghats Portal — it is the thing that makes an email from you worth answering.
- **It survives you.** A DOI'd deposit outlives a website by design, and the corpus established that scholarly links have roughly a 14-year half-life with only 62% archived. The dataset is the durable part; the site is not.
- **It is 6–12 months, not 10–16 years.** The corpus is unambiguous that a full multi-domain atlas takes a decade or more, with no counterexample. A gazetteer dataset does not.

**Do that before the PBR work**, tempting as the 272,648 registers are. The gazetteer is smaller, has no consent problem, and gives you the standing to approach the PBR question properly — which is a benefit-sharing negotiation with communities before it is a data project.

One caution, offered because you asked for evidence rather than encouragement and this follows from it: the corpus shows this work rewards patience over decades and punishes nothing so much as the maintainer stopping. Before committing years, it is worth being honest with yourself about whether you want this because the forests matter to you, or because a large well-defined problem is satisfying to work on. Both are legitimate. Only the first survives year seven, when the novelty is gone and nobody is watching. Everything in the corpus that lasted was made by someone in the first category.
