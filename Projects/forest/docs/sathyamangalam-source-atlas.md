# Sathyamangalam — The Source Atlas
## Every findable record, oldest to newest, and what each one yields

**Compiled:** 13 August 2026 · **For:** the Sathyamangalam open atlas project
**Purpose:** the complete inventory of source material that exists online for the Sathyamangalam landscape — what it contains, where it is, whether it is legally reusable, and what it unlocks for the site.

---

## The headline finds

Four discoveries change the scope of this project.

**1. The 391-page Management Plan for Sathyamangalam Wildlife Sanctuary, 2010–2020.** Publicly readable on Academia.edu. It contains range-by-range and beat-by-beat area figures, Champion & Seth forest-type classifications, ~150 tree species, 77+ herb species, grass lists, 40+ mammal species, 225+ bird species, butterfly appendices, 40+ named streams and 25+ ponds, the full working-circle history back to the sandalwood depot of 1919, cattle and grazing figures, village inventories, and maps. **This single document can populate half the site's reference content.** Nothing on the public internet has extracted it.

**2. The Battle of Sathyamangalam, 13–15 September 1790.** Tipu Sultan descended the Gajalhatti Pass on 9 September and attacked Captain John Floyd's East India Company force at Sathyamangalam. The British took ~500 casualties and Floyd retreated to Coimbatore; Mysore lost the commander Burhan-ud-din. It is catalogued in English under corrupted spellings — *Sittimungulum*, *Sattiamungulum*, *Sathinungulum* — which is precisely why nobody covering Sathyamangalam has connected it. **A significant Anglo-Mysore War battle happened here and the internet has effectively lost it.**

**3. The colonial gazetteers are online in full text, free.** Nicholson's *Manual of the Coimbatore District* (1887) and the *Madras District Gazetteers: Coimbatore* (W. Francis, 1908) are both on the Internet Archive as searchable text. Nicholson already yields: Satyamangalam taluk at **1,174 square miles**, **184 inhabited villages and 1 town**, the Bhavani running **105 miles** through Satyamangalam and Bhavani taluks, sandalwood "spread over the whole tract," and the Sholagars described as "an almost wild race (shola == forest) living on the hills."

**4. Survey of India one-inch maps, 1870–1966, are digitised and georeferenced by the National Library of Scotland.** You can overlay 19th-century forest, village and track layers on your own Sentinel-2 rasters. **A century-and-a-half land-cover change analysis for this landscape is available to anyone who bothers — and nobody has.**

# PART 1 — The deep record (pre-1900)

## 1.1 Prehistory and archaeology

| Source | What it yields | Access |
|---|---|---|
| Madras District Gazetteer, Coimbatore (1908), early chapters | Explicitly covers "the Neolithic Age through Ganga rule" for the district | archive.org full text, free |
| Megalithic sites of the Bhavani and Moyar valleys | Cairn circles, urn burials — Kongu region is dense with them; specific STR-tract sites are poorly published | ASI reports; Tamil Nadu Dept. of Archaeology; needs field enquiry |
| Rock art / rock shelters | Not yet confirmed for the STR tract in published sources; adjacent Eastern Ghats sites are documented. **Genuine open question — worth asking the department directly.** | — |

**Assessment:** this is the weakest-documented layer and therefore the highest-value original contribution. Even assembling what *is* known would be a first.

## 1.2 Dynastic and epigraphic

Chera rule, then Vijayanagara, then Mysore, then Tipu Sultan, then the British. The name itself is Tamil — *Sathya* (truth) + *mangalam* (auspicious place). Temple inscriptions in the Bhavani–Moyar corridor are the primary evidence base.

- **Annual Reports on Indian Epigraphy / South Indian Inscriptions** — the standard corpus; searchable volumes on archive.org.
- **Bannari Amman Temple** — major regional pilgrimage site at the foot of the ghat; foundation history is locally transmitted rather than published. A documented oral-history piece here would be genuinely new.
- **Vijayamangalam Jain temple** (Erode district) — evidence of the region's Jain layer, usually omitted from Sathyamangalam accounts entirely.

## 1.3 The Anglo-Mysore Wars — the lost battle

**Battle of Sathyamangalam, 13–15 September 1790**, Third Anglo-Mysore War.

- Tipu Sultan descended the **Gajalhatti Pass on 9 September 1790**
- Attacked **Captain John Floyd's** EIC contingent at Sathyamangalam
- Tipu departed mid-engagement; **Kamar-ud-Din Khan** assumed command
- British casualties ~**500**; Floyd retreated to Coimbatore
- Mysore lost **Burhan-ud-din**, described as one of Tipu's ablest commanders

**Why it matters for the site:** the Dimbam ghat and the Gajalhatti pass were strategic military corridors two centuries before they were tourist roads. That reframes the whole landscape. Search the primary literature under every spelling variant — *Sittimungulum*, *Sattiamungulum*, *Sathinungulum*, *Satyamanagalam* — in Wilks's *Historical Sketches of the South of India*, Mark Wood's campaign maps, and the EIC military dispatches on archive.org and Google Books.

## 1.4 Buchanan, 1807 — the first scientific survey

Francis Buchanan's *A Journey from Madras through the Countries of Mysore, Canara, and Malabar* (1807) was commissioned by Wellesley expressly to record "agriculture, arts, and commerce; the religion, manners, and customs; the history natural and civil, and antiquities." He travelled this corridor. **This is the oldest systematic description of the landscape's ecology and people in any language, and it is free.**

- Volume 1: archive.org — `journeyfrommadra01hami`
- Volume 2: Heidelberg digitised — `digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/buchanan1807bd2`
- Also: Wellcome Collection, Royal Collection Trust

**Action:** download all volumes, grep for *Satyamangalam*, *Danaiken kottai*, *Gajalhatti*, *Bhavani*, *Sholaga*, *sandal*. Any passage found is a genuine primary-source scoop.

**Already extracted from Nicholson (1887):**
- Satyamangalam taluk: **1,174 sq miles**, **184 inhabited villages**, **1 town**
- The Bhavani, "a perennial river rising on the north-west of the district," traverses **105 miles** through Satyamangalam and Bhavani taluks

> **Method note.** These full-text files are very large and web-fetch tools truncate them. Download the `_djvu.txt` files directly and grep locally for every spelling variant — *Satyamangalam, Sathyamangalam, Sattiamangalam, Talamalai, Talaimalai, Bargur, Hasanur, Hassanur, Danaiken kottai, Gajalhatti, Gejjalhatti, Moyar, Mayar, Sholaga, Sholagar, Irular, Kurumbar*. Expect several thousand words of usable primary material per volume. **This is the single highest-yield task in this document.**

## 1.6 Colonial forest administration

- **Madras Forest Act, 1882** — the legal instrument under which these blocks were first reserved.
- **Madras Forest Department** formed 1856; the tract was administered by **one range officer and eight guards**.
- **Working plans for Sathyamangalam Forest Division** — the direct ancestors of today's management plan. Historic working plans are held by the Tamil Nadu Forest Department and, in part, at the **Gass Forest Museum, Coimbatore** (established 1902), which holds Madras Forest Department historical records, specimens and library material. **Physically reachable from Erode. Go.**
- **Sandalwood Working Circle** — the 2010 management plan records that sandalwood has been protected here since the Tipu Sultan era, with a **depot established in 1919**.
- **Annual Administration Reports of the Forest Department, Madras Presidency** — decades of felling, revenue, fire and wildlife data. Partially on archive.org; the rest at Tamil Nadu Archives.

**The flagship analysis this unlocks:** georeference the 1870s–1920s one-inch sheets, overlay current Sentinel-2 land cover, and publish a **150-year forest-change map of the Sathyamangalam tract**. Village sites that vanished, tracks that became NH-948, forest boundaries that moved, the Moyar's channel. Nobody has done this for this landscape. It is directly within your existing GIS skill set, and it would be the most cited thing on the site.

# PART 2 — The twentieth century

## 2.1 The Veerappan era, 1970s–2004

The single most-searched thing about this forest, and the worst-served by serious sources.

- **Mongabay India (June 2019)** — "Old Veerappan lair set to be a tiger den": the conservation framing.
- **Court records and Special Task Force accounts** — the Karnataka/Tamil Nadu joint STF; Operation Cocoon (October 2004).
- **The 1997 Kannada film-star kidnapping** and the sandalwood/ivory economy.
- **Ecological consequence:** three decades of effective ungovernability. The reserve's recovery timeline begins after October 2004 — that causal link is central to the site's history section and is rarely stated plainly.
- **Caution:** vast quantities of sensationalised, uncited material online. Use court documents, contemporaneous newspaper archives (The Hindu, Indian Express) and Mongabay only.

# PART 3 — The management plan (the single richest document)

**TAMILNADU FOREST DEPARTMENT MANAGEMENT PLAN FOR SATHYAMANGALAM WILDLIFE SANCTUARY (2010 TO 2020)** — 391 pages, on Academia.edu.

### Administrative geography — extractable directly

| Range | Area (ha) | Key beats |
|---|---|---|
| Sathyamangalam | 16,700.65 | Dimbam, Vadavalli, Chickarasampalayam, Kondappanaickenpalayam, Kembanaikenpalayam |
| Bhavanisagar | 14,024.65 | Thengumarahada, Talamalai (pt), Gejalatti (pt), Peerkadavu (pt), Kothamangalam, Bannari (pt) |
| Talavadi | 8,300.98 | Palayam, Belathur, Geddavady |
| T.N. Palayam | 13,408.66 | Kadambur East (pt), Vilankombai, Kongarpalayam, Kovilur (pt), Bungalowpudur, Kanankampalayam, Gundri (pt) |

Coordinates of the sanctuary: **11°29'15"–11°48'41"N, 76°50'–77°27'22"E**. Parent Sathyamangalam Forest Division: **1,455 km²**.

### Forest types, properly classified (Champion & Seth 1968)

- **6A/C1** Southern Tropical Dry Thorn — ~600 mm
- **5A/C3** Southern Tropical Dry Mixed Deciduous — 800–850 mm; *Anogeissus latifolia*, *Terminalia crenulata* dominant
- **8A/C1** Southern Sub-Tropical Hill Forest — *Acacia*, *Albizia*, *Hardwickia*
- **2A/C2** Southern Tropical Semi-Evergreen — moist pockets ~1,200 m
- **Riparian** — Moyar corridor; *Terminalia arjuna*, *Spondias mangifera*, bamboo

### Climate — the numbers Wikipedia is missing entirely

- **Average annual rainfall: 824 mm**
- **Northeast monsoon supplies 70%** (September–November)
- Temperature: **21.54–27.02 °C** on the plateau; **26.24–32.84 °C** on the plains
- Dry period January–April; **March–April critical** for water stress and fire

### Biological inventory in the appendices

~150 tree species · shrub list · **77+ herb species** · grasses (*Themeda triandra*, *Heteropogon*, *Digitaria*) · **40+ mammals** · **225+ birds** · **15+ amphibians** · fish including mahseer of **30–40 kg** · a butterfly appendix · 40+ named streams · 25+ ponds.

### Baseline fauna figures (2009–10) — the "before" picture

- Tigers: **8–10** (2009 scat DNA analysis, CCMB Hyderabad); WWF camera traps at 35 Moyar valley locations captured **6 tigers** regularly
- Elephants: **350–450** in the sanctuary; **850** in the 2009 synchronised census
- Leopards: **~20** · Blackbuck: **800–900** · Four-horned antelope: **~100**
- Vultures: white-backed, king, long-billed, neophron

**Set against today's 112 tigers and 651 elephants, this is the most powerful data story the site has** — and it comes from the department's own document, not from anyone's interpretation.

### Working-circle history

Sandalwood (protected since Tipu's era; depot 1919) · fuelwood (pre-1975) · bamboo (until 1981; gregarious flowering 1972–75 destroyed most stocks) · selection felling coupes (stopped 1980, cited as causing habitat degradation) · minor forest produce. NTFP revenue **₹60–80 lakh/year**, now routed to communities through Village Forest Committees.

### Pressures, quantified

~**25,000 cattle** grazing, carrying anthrax, foot-and-mouth and haemorrhagic septicaemia · fire in March–April · encroachments · *Prosopis juliflora* and *Lantana camara* as the named invasives of 2010 (note: *Senna spectabilis* had not yet become the headline problem — **that shift over fifteen years is itself a finding**) · elephant electrocution · corridor loss.

### Communities

Sholagas, Irulas, Ooralis, Kurumbas. **136 villages** in the zone of influence; **138** bordering forest; **150** abutting the boundary. Eco-Development Committees in 136 fringe villages.

> **Priority action:** obtain the full PDF and systematically extract every appendix. This is weeks of work and it is worth more than any other single task in the project.

## 5.2 Images — the honest position

You asked for a world-class site with proper images. Here is the truth: **the free image supply for Sathyamangalam specifically is thin, and that is a problem you must solve with a camera, not a search engine.**

**Legally usable now:**
- **iNaturalist** — CC-licensed species photographs, many from this landscape. Best available source for species entries. Attribution required; check each licence (CC BY vs CC BY-NC).
- **Wikimedia Commons** — some Nilgiri/Moyar/Bhavanisagar imagery; STR-specific coverage is sparse.
- **Flickr CC search** — filtered to CC BY / CC BY-SA.
- **British Library / India Office photographs** (`imagesonline.bl.uk`, and the Commons category) — 19th-century Madras Presidency photographs, public domain. Historic sandalwood, forest and hill-tribe imagery for the history section.
- **PICRYL** — public domain aggregator including Curzon Collection material.
- **Survey of India historical map sheets (NLS)** — beautiful as full-bleed section backgrounds, and unusual.
- **Sentinel-2 and Landsat imagery** — free, and a false-colour Moyar gorge or a *Senna* flowering NDVI composite is a striking, genuinely unique visual nobody else has.

**The gap, stated plainly:** there is no free library of high-quality photographs of the Sathyamangalam landscape, its species and its people. Every travel site uses the same handful of stock images, several of which are not even from this reserve.

**What to do about it:**
1. **Shoot it yourself.** You live inside the landscape. A year of weekend fieldwork — Dimbam mist, the Moyar gorge, Bhavanisagar backwaters, *Senna* in yellow flower, the Talamalai plateau, the Bannari temple, ghat hairpins — gives you an image library nobody can match and which becomes an asset in its own right.
2. **Commission or partner with local photographers** for credit and licence rather than cash.
3. **Contributor pipeline:** naturalists, guides and forest watchers submit photographs under a clear licence with permanent attribution.
4. **Community photography with consent** — never stock imagery of tribal people; that is both ethically wrong and instantly visible as fake.
5. **Lean on what's unique to you:** satellite composites, georeferenced historical maps, data visualisation, and typography. A site that looks world-class on the strength of its *data* rather than borrowed photographs is more defensible and more honest.

## 5.3 Archives to visit in person

- **Gass Forest Museum, Coimbatore** (est. 1902) — Madras Forest Department historical records, specimens, library. Two hours from Erode.
- **Tamil Nadu Archives, Chennai** — Government Orders, district records, forest administration reports.
- **Roja Muthiah Research Library, Chennai** — Tamil-language print archive; local histories, temple literature, periodicals.
- **Endangered Archives Programme, British Library** — EAP458 (Tamil agrarian history, colonial period), EAP314 (Tamil customary law, village judicial assemblies 1870–1940). Free online.
- **STR Field Director's office, Sathyamangalam** — working plans, compartment maps, unpublished census data. The one that matters most and the one that needs a relationship, not a download.
