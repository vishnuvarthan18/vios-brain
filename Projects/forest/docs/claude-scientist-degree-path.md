| Programme | Does it accept Computer Science? |

## 2. What "scientist" actually needs

The real permanent scientist job is **Scientist-B** at ICFRE (the Indian Council of Forestry Research and Education, which runs IFGTB Coimbatore, FRI Dehradun, and other institutes).

Here is what their 2025 recruitment actually asked for:

- **First Class Master's degree** in the relevant subject
- Age 21 to 35 (you are 23–24, so you have roughly 11 years of runway)
- **PhD is NOT required.** It is only "desirable"
- **NET is NOT required**
- Pay: Rs 56,100 to 1,77,500 (Level 10), permanent central government

So the whole path is one sentence: **get a first-class Master's in a wildlife or forestry subject, then apply for Scientist-B.**

That is it. No IFoS exam. No PhD. No 10-year wait.

## 3. Your three real doors

### Door 1 — NCBS, Bangalore (best quality, free)

- **Eligibility:** Bachelor's in *any* discipline, 50% in core subjects
- **Age limit:** None found
- **Cost:** Free. They pay full tuition, give hostel, and give research project grants
- **Runs:** Every 2 years only
- **Next intake:** The 2026–28 batch already started. Next one is 2028–30, advertised around **September 2027**, entrance exam around **December 2027**
- **Selection:** Written test (general aptitude, English, basic biology and ecology) + application + reference letters + interview
- **Downside:** Very competitive, small batch, and you wait until 2028 to start

### Door 2 — WII, Dehradun (fastest, but the clock is running)

- **Eligibility:** Computer Science explicitly listed. 50% aggregate (45% SC/ST)
- **Age limit:** **25 years** as of 31 March of the application year. 28 for OBC, 30 for SC/ST
- **Cost:** About Rs 6,50,000 for the two years. But **8 full merit scholarships** are given to Indian students, plus a Rs 3,000/month stipend
- **Runs:** Every year
- **Next intake:** Registration usually opens **February**, closes **end of March**, entrance test in **April**, course starts **August**
- **Selection:** Online national test (2 hours, 100 marks) + interview at Dehradun. Final score is 50% test + 50% interview

> **This is the urgent one.** If you are General category, the 25-year age cap means the **February–March 2027 window is probably your last chance**. Check your exact date of birth against 31 March 2027 today.

### Door 3 — SACON, Anaikatty (closest to home)

- M.Sc. Wildlife Science & Ornithology, 45 minutes from Coimbatore
- Also a recognised PhD centre through Bharathiar University
- Eligibility not published clearly online. **Call or email them directly**

Also nearby: Kongunadu Arts and Science College, Coimbatore runs an M.Sc. Wildlife Biology. Their eligibility list is 30 biology-side subjects and Computer Science does not appear to be on it. Worth one phone call to confirm, but do not count on it.

## 4. The plan

### Now to February 2027 — prepare and build

1. **Check your date of birth against the WII age cap.** Do this first. It decides whether Door 2 is open.
2. **Study for the entrance test.** Both WII and NCBS test basic biology, ecology, conservation issues, English and aptitude. You have no biology background, so this is the real work. Give it 1–2 hours a day for a year. Standard starting texts: any undergraduate ecology textbook, plus the Wildlife Protection Act basics and current Indian conservation news.
3. **Keep the studio running.** It pays for everything and you will need it during the degree.
4. **Build the portfolio anyway.** The Senna/Lantana mapping project, eBird and iNaturalist records, one open-source contribution. This helps the NCBS application directly, because they read your application and take references.
5. **Get real field time.** Volunteer with Keystone Foundation in Kotagiri or a forest department census. Interviewers at both places ask why you want this, and having been in the forest is a better answer than wanting to be in the forest.

### February–March 2027 — apply to WII

Register, sit the April test, attend the Dehradun interview. If you get in with a scholarship, start August 2027.

### September–December 2027 — apply to NCBS

Run this in parallel regardless of the WII result. It is free and it is the better programme.

### 2027 to 2029 — do the Master's

Two years, full time, residential. This is the part where you cannot also run a business at full speed. Plan the studio to run on autopilot or hand it to someone.

### 2029 onwards — become the scientist

- Apply for ICFRE Scientist-B when a discipline matching your specialisation opens
- Or project scientist roles at WII, SACON, FSI, which you will now be over-qualified for rather than under
- Or PhD, if you want the research career rather than the government post
- Your CS skills become a rare advantage here, not a handicap. Almost nobody in Indian wildlife science can build a model and a map. You will be able to.

## 5. The honest costs

**Money.** WII is Rs 6.5 lakh unless you win a scholarship. NCBS is free. Two years of reduced studio income on top of that.

**Time.** You start the degree at 25, finish at 27, and are competing for Scientist-B posts from 28. That is normal. Most people in these roles took a similar route.

**Risk.** Both entrances are competitive and you may not clear on the first try. WII you can retake only while under the age cap. NCBS you can retake in 2030 with no age limit, which makes NCBS the safer long-term door.

**The exam you said you did not want.** These entrance tests are exams. They are not UPSC-style multi-year grinds, but they are exams. If that is a hard no, the degree path closes and you are back to the grant-and-consultancy plan.
