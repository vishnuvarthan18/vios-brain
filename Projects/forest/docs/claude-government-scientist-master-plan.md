## Part 1 — The bad news, first

You need to hear this before anything else.

**WII (Wildlife Institute of India) does not recruit permanent Scientists from the open market.**

Their scientist vacancies are Scientist-D, E, F and G, and they are **deputation only** — reserved for IFS and State Forest Service officers already in government. Everything else at WII is contract project staff.

**SACON is the same.** No permanent Scientist post has been openly advertised in any cycle we could find. Only project contracts.

**FSI is the same.** No permanent scientist cadre entry. Their "Technical Associate" is contractual.

So the picture in your head — "become a scientist at WII" — is not a door that exists for outsiders. Stop aiming at it.

**The permanent government scientist jobs that are real and open to you are these:**

| Post | Where | Pay |
|---|---|---|
| **ICFRE Scientist-B** | Forest research institutes, including IFGTB Coimbatore | Level 10, ₹56,100 – 1,77,500 |
| **CPCB Scientist-B** | Central Pollution Control Board | Level 10, ₹56,100 – 1,77,500 |
| **TNPCB Environmental Scientist** | Tamil Nadu, state post | ₹37,700 – 1,19,500 |
| GBPNIHE Almora Scientist-B | Himalaya | Level 10 |
| NIH Roorkee Scientist-B | Water and hydrology | Level 10 |

These are the target. ICFRE is the forest one. CPCB is the most regular.

## Part 2 — The good news

Now the part that actually favours you.

### No PhD needed

Every one of those posts asks for a **Master's degree**. PhD is listed only as "desirable" — and at ICFRE, having a PhD is worth about 3.5 marks out of 470. Practically nothing.

### No NET needed

**Not one of these ten routes requires UGC or CSIR NET.** This is the single most useful fact in this document. NET is "desirable" at CPCB and NIH Roorkee. Nowhere is it mandatory.

### Age limit 35, and you are 24

You have **eleven years**. ICFRE runs a cycle roughly every 3 years. CPCB every 2–3 years. That is around 3–4 ICFRE chances and 4–5 CPCB chances before you age out in about 2037.

You are not late. You are early.

### The exam is a subject exam, not UPSC

The ICFRE written test:

- 100 objective questions, 400 marks, 2 hours
- **Only your subject.** No general knowledge, no current affairs, no essay
- +4 for correct, −1 for wrong
- Syllabus is published on their website, discipline by discipline
- Qualifying mark: 50% general, 45% OBC, 40% SC/ST
- Top 5 candidates per vacancy go to interview
- Final score = written (400) + interview (70). So **85% of your fate is the written exam**

This is a fair, studyable exam. Not a lottery, not a connections game. You revise a published syllabus and you sit a paper. That suits you.

## Part 3 — Which Master's to do

This is the most important decision in the whole plan. Pick wrong and you lock yourself out of most posts.

### The answer: M.Sc. Environmental Science

Here is why it beats every alternative:

| Master's subject | Which posts it opens |
|---|---|
| **Environmental Science** | **CPCB, TNPCB, ICFRE (Forest Ecology & Environment Management), GBPNIHE, UPSC Scientist-B posts** |
| Forestry | ICFRE only (but 4 dedicated posts there) |

Environmental Science opens the most doors. It is the correct choice.

**Note this carefully:** M.Sc. Wildlife Science, the degree you were most excited about, is the *worst* one for a government scientist career. It is a great degree for NGO and research work. It is a dead end for permanent government posts, because the two institutes that would hire for it do not hire from outside.

## Part 4 — The trap that could ruin this

**Read this twice.**

ICFRE's advertisement says, word for word:

> "Any percentage of marks or Grade Point Average (GPA) will not be considered as first class unless first class is mentioned in degree or marksheet."

So it is not enough to score 65%. **The words "First Class" must be physically printed on your degree certificate or marksheet.**

Many distance universities issue grade-based marksheets that never print those words. If IGNOU does that, your ₹17,000 and two years buy you a degree that gets rejected at screening.

**Before you pay a single rupee to IGNOU, do this:**

Write to IGNOU — email, not phone, so you have it in writing — and ask exactly this:

> "Does the M.Sc. Environmental Science degree certificate or marksheet print the words 'First Class' for students who score above 60%? I need this for a Government of India Scientist-B application which requires 'First Class' to be mentioned on the degree."

Keep the reply. If the answer is no, or vague, **do a regular M.Sc. instead** — Bharathiar University Coimbatore, Periyar University Salem, or a nearby college, in evening or weekend mode if available.

Two other things not confirmed anywhere:

- No ICFRE document says distance degrees are accepted. No document says they are barred either. It is silent. Send them a written query too: **recruitment.icfre.gov.in** contact, or ICFRE HQ Dehradun.
- Check whether your own **B.Sc. marksheet** says "First Class". Some posts look at the bachelor's too.

If distance turns out to be risky, the safe version is a **regular M.Sc. from a Tamil Nadu university**, which costs more time but removes all doubt.

## Part 7 — Two important warnings

**One: posting is assigned, not chosen.**

ICFRE says every post carries liability to serve at any institute in India. There is no way to apply specifically to IFGTB Coimbatore. You could be posted to Jodhpur, Jorhat, Shimla or Jabalpur. If staying near Erode is non-negotiable, TNPCB is the only state-level option, and it recruits rarely.

**Two: these posts are shrinking.**

ICFRE Scientist-B post counts: 54 in 2019, 44 in 2022, 25 in 2025. The trend is down. Do not treat this as a guaranteed destination. Keep the NGO and consultancy route alive in parallel — that is what the GitHub project, the Keystone volunteering and the WILDLABS presence are for.
