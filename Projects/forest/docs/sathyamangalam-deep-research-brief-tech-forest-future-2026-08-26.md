# Deep research brief — does "technology + forest/conservation" have a real future as a field to dedicate a life to

For Vishnu to run in a separate Claude Cowork session. **Purpose: this is not a job search. I am deciding whether to dedicate my life's work — as a builder, a researcher, or both, the shape is not yet decided — to the intersection of technology and forest/conservation, in India and globally, starting from the Sathyamangalam atlas but not limited to it. I need to know if this field itself has a real, growing future, backed by hard evidence, before committing years to it. This is not about finding an employer or a salary — it is about whether the field is real, where it is heading, and what shape a life's work in it could actually take.**

## Depth and format requirements — read before starting

- **Every claim about growth or importance needs a number, a year, and a source.** If no solid evidence exists for a claim, say so explicitly — do not soften it into vague optimism.
- **Do not frame this around jobs, salaries, or employers.** This is about the field's trajectory and the shape of a life's work in it — funding, institutions, research direction, and what independent builders/researchers in this space actually do, not who is hiring.
- Use tables wherever comparing across years, countries, or examples.
- Every section ends with its own sources list.

## Context to paste in

I am a CS graduate in Erode, Tamil Nadu, India, currently building a landscape-scale conservation data project for a tiger reserve (species data, historical documents, legal records, a disputed-facts feature). I am now asking a bigger question: should "technology + forest/conservation" — geoinformatics, remote sensing, ecological data systems, biodiversity informatics — be my life's work, across India and potentially globally, either as someone who builds tools and platforms in this space, or as someone who does independent research and becomes a recognised voice in it, or both? I am not looking for a job. I want to know if this field is real, growing, and has room for an independent person to matter in it — with evidence, not encouragement.

## What I need, strictly in this order

### Task 1 (do first) — Is this a real, growing field, with evidence?
Establish the trajectory of technology applied to forestry/conservation as a field, using real indicators:
- Government commitment and scale: India's actual investment and programme growth in remote sensing/GIS for forestry and conservation over time (ISRO/NRSC/Bhuvan, Forest Survey of India, MoEFCC digital initiatives) — real figures and how they've changed year over year, not a single snapshot.
- Global institutional commitment: is spending, programme count, or stated priority from major conservation/environmental bodies (UN, World Bank, GBIF, IUCN, major foundations) on data/technology infrastructure for conservation increasing, flat, or decreasing — with actual figures where publishable.
- Scientific and technical maturity: is the volume of published research, new open-source tools, or new standards in this space (biodiversity informatics, conservation GIS, ecological data science) growing — publication counts, new major tools/standards launched per year, conference growth (e.g. TDWG/BISS attendance or submission counts over time).
- Any credible long-range forecast (10+ years) from a government, UN, or major research body on the role of data/technology in conservation and forestry specifically.

### Task 2 — Where does an independent person actually fit, and has anyone done this before?
- Find real examples — anywhere in the world, but especially India — of individuals or very small teams (not large institutions) who built a lasting body of work, a tool, a dataset, or a research reputation at this intersection, independently or semi-independently, and succeeded. What did their path actually look like — did they start solo and get absorbed by an institution, stay independent long-term, or something else?
- Find the honest counter-examples too: people who tried this independently and it did not work out, and why, as far as can be determined.
- Is there a recognisable, respected category of person who does this — "conservation technologist," "biodiversity informatician," independent ecological-data researcher — with actual visible careers, or is this still too new/undefined a role for that to exist yet?

### Task 3 — What does sustaining this actually look like, for an independent person or small team, long-term?
Money matters enough to research, but only as a means to keep researching/building, not as the goal.
- What funding mechanisms actually exist for an independent person or very small team doing technology-for-conservation work long-term — research grants, fellowships, government schemes (India-specific and global), foundation funding, open-source/public-infrastructure funding models — with real figures on typical amounts and how competitive/accessible they are to someone without an institutional affiliation yet.
- Are there any funding routes specifically for early-stage independent people (not established institutions) in this space, in India or globally?
- What is the realistic minimum sustainable setup — is there a documented case of someone running this kind of work on a very small or zero budget long-term, and what actually made that possible?

### Task 4 — What would the next 10–20 years of this field plausibly look like?
- Based on Tasks 1–3, what are the most credible near-term (India specifically) and long-term (global) developments likely to shape this field — new technology (satellite data becoming cheaper/more available, AI applied to ecological monitoring, etc.), new policy drivers (climate commitments, biodiversity targets like the Kunming-Montreal framework), or new institutional structures?
- Is there a specific gap or emerging need in this field, in India specifically, that an independent person could plausibly be early to and become known for — not a generic "there's always a need," but something concrete and evidenced by what's covered in Tasks 1–3.

### Task 5 — Synthesis: a direct verdict
- State plainly: does the evidence support treating "technology + forest/conservation" as a field with real, durable growth worth dedicating a life to — as a builder, a researcher, or both? Give a confidence level and say exactly what it rests on.
- If yes: what is the single most credible next move — not a job application, but a concrete next building or research step — that would put someone in the strongest position to matter in this field over the next 5–10 years.
- If the evidence is mixed or thin in places, say exactly where, rather than papering over it with encouragement.

## Deliverable format

One report, Tasks 1–5 in order, each with its own sources list. Every claim about growth or trajectory needs a number, a year, and a citation. End with a direct, unambiguous verdict in Task 5 — not a list of "it depends" factors.

This comes back for a decision about direction, not for further research.
