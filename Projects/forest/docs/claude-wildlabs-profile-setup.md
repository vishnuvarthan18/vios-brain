## PART 3 — The visibility plan (this is what actually makes you known)

Filling the profile gets you found. This gets you known.

### Week 1 — Arrive properly
- Complete every profile field above and join the groups.
- Post one introduction in the **Geospatial** group. Not "hello, I'm new." Title it as a question with a place name in it:
  `Verifying post-clearance Senna regrowth with Sentinel-2 — has anyone solved the flowering-phenology confound?`
  Two paragraphs: what you're building, what specific problem you're stuck on, what data you have. Ending on a real technical question is what gets replies.
- Comment substantively on three existing threads. Substantive = you add a method, a paper, a dataset, or a correction. Never "great post."

### Weeks 2–6 — Publish the pilot
- Ship the GEE + Sentinel-2 change-detection notebook to public GitHub. README with a map image at the top.
- Write it up as a WILDLABS **article** (not just a discussion post). Articles are what earn contributor visibility and get shared in the newsletter.
- Structure: the problem (everyone counts hectares cleared, nobody verifies regrowth) → the method → what worked → what failed → what you need help with.
- Being honest about what failed is what makes technical people in this community engage.

### Months 2–6 — Become the person who answers
- Answer every new question in the Geospatial group that touches Sentinel-2, GEE, QGIS or invasive mapping. Consistency beats brilliance here.
- One article every 4–6 weeks. Cadence matters more than length.
- Apply to speak at a WILDLABS **Variety Hour** — they actively want non-Western practitioners and the bar is a real project, not a PhD. That's how you get the "Event Speaker" badge on your profile.
- Apply for a **WILDLABS Award** ($10k / $50k tiers, open to independent developers). Your invasive-mapping pilot is a direct fit.

### The metric
Not followers. The measure is: when someone in the Nilgiris landscape has an invasive-mapping question, does your name come up? Three named people replying to your threads by month 6 is the real benchmark.

## PART 4 — Why this works

- WILDLABS has ~6,000 members across 120 countries and very few active Indians. The Geospatial and Acoustics groups are dominated by North American and European practitioners.
- Hyper-local specificity is your entire edge. "Geospatial analyst, India" is invisible. "Post-clearance Senna verification in the Sathyamangalam–Mudumalai corridor" has no competition anywhere on the platform.
- The people you actually need — Keystone Foundation, NCF, ATREE, Technology for Wildlife, SACON — read this platform. A visible WILDLABS track record is a warmer introduction than a cold email, and it's the same work either way.
