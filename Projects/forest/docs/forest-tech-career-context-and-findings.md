## 4. Eligibility Findings (verified)

### Blocked
- **UPSC Indian Forest Service (IFoS).** Requires a Bachelor's with at least one of: Animal Husbandry and Veterinary Science, Botany, Chemistry, Geology, Mathematics, Physics, Statistics, Zoology, OR a Bachelor's in Agriculture, Forestry or Engineering. **B.Sc. Computer Science is not on the list.** A B.E./B.Tech in CS would have counted as Engineering.
- **UPPSC ACF (Uttar Pradesh).** Computer Science not listed.
- **WII Project Associate-I and above.** Requires a 4-year Bachelor's or a Master's. A 3-year B.Sc. falls short. Project Assistant tier remains open.

### Open
- **TNPSC Assistant Conservator of Forests (Group IA).** Essential qualification explicitly includes Computer Applications and Computer Science. Max age 34 (general). Permanent, gazetted, field. **But the 2025 notification carried only 2 vacancies.** Treat as a lottery ticket, not a plan. Mains includes a qualifying Tamil paper. Physical: height, chest, endurance walk before interview.
- **TNFUSRC Forester.** B.Sc. or Engineering, with Computer Applications on the eligible list. Permanent, uniformed, forest, field. Far more posts (one TNFUSRC round covered 1,382 vacancies across Forest Guard, Watcher and Forester). Physical test is the real filter.
- **RPSC Rajasthan ACF and FRO.** Computer Application/Science listed as a qualifying subject AND available as an exam optional. Age limit 40. Cost: Hindi Devanagari requirement and Rajasthani culture component. Height 163 cm men, 150 cm women.
- **DRDO CEPTAM STA-B.** B.Sc. in Computer Science accepted. Permanent central government, technical. CEPTAM 11 carried 561 STA-B vacancies. **Age limit 28**, and CEPTAM runs only every few years (CEPTAM 11 applications closed January 2026), so the next window may land near the cut-off. DGRE postings put you in the Himalaya.
- **Project posts by direct interview** at WII, SACON, ICFRE, FSI, GBPNIHE, CSIR institutes. Shortlist plus interview, no written exam. Project Assistant tier open now with a B.Sc.

### Physical standards to self-check before anything else
- Rajasthan: 163 cm men, 150 cm women.
- Andhra Pradesh FRO: men 25 km in 4 hours, women 16 km in 4 hours.
- TNPSC ACF: height, chest, endurance walk before interview.
- TNFUSRC Forester: height, chest, medical.

Measure height and chest. Time a 10 km walk. If height blocks you, the uniformed routes close and the tech/NGO routes stay open.

## 6. Seven Under-Addressed Technical Gaps

### Gap 1: Multi-reserve invasive species time-series mapping (strongest opportunity)

**Situation.** Senna spectabilis has spread across the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve. In Mudumalai, spread estimated at 1,500 hectares in recent years, with roughly 400 hectares cleared from January 2024. Tamil Nadu has cleared 356.50 hectares inside Sathyamangalam. The Madras High Court ordered removal from both Mudumalai and Sathyamangalam, with a state target of complete removal from TN forests by 1 October 2025. Court proceedings referenced roughly 28,000 tonnes remaining statewide, split between TNPL and Seshasayee Papers, at a conservation charge of Rs 350 per tonne.

The TN PIPER Policy (Policy on Invasive Plants and Ecological Restoration, announced 3 September 2021, operational 2022-23) is the first state-level framework of its kind. Lantana removal runs across all forest divisions at 50 hectares/month since September 2024, rising to 75 hectares/month in severe protected areas from January 2025. By 28 February 2023, 2,969.29 hectares had been cleared statewide.

**What is unsolved.**
- No public, continuously updated, multi-reserve invasion map stitching Mudumalai, Sathyamangalam, Bandipur, Nagarhole, Wayanad and BRT into one layer. MTR's Field Director stated landscape-wide coordinated removal is imperative because the species reinvades from adjacent reserves.
- **Post-clearance regrowth verification is the live, unmet need right now.** Everyone counts hectares cleared. Nobody systematically verifies what grew back.
- Existing work stops short of an operational tool. The Moyar-Bhavani citizen-science invasive atlas (Keystone Foundation and ATREE) was an explicit pilot shortlisting 26 species. A 2020 GEE study on NBR covered land-cover degradation, not species-level invasion time series. A 2025 Biological Invasions paper used species-distribution models, not live mapping.
- The invasive-biomass economy (Lantana briquetting, Senna pulp to TNPL) has no public spatial or logistics tooling.

**Why you can do it.** Senna's bright yellow flowering canopy is a strong spectral and phenological signal. Free Sentinel-2 imagery plus Google Earth Engine gives you multi-year change detection with no hardware.

**First project.** A GEE + Sentinel-2 change-detection notebook mapping Senna/Lantana flowering signals across two or three adjoining reserves, flagging post-clearance regrowth compartment by compartment. Publish as an open QGIS/GeoPandas workflow with a simple web map. Compare pixel-based and object-based classification and document which wins for this canopy, because the literature has not settled it here.

### Gap 2: Drone thermal and LiDAR data processing (Tamil Nadu specific)

In late May 2025 the TN Forest Department, via the TBGPCCR project, deployed drones with 48 MP cameras, thermal sensors and GPS across **13 territorial forest circles** for elephant tracking, fire management and anti-poaching. Only three staff per circle are being trained, **as pilots**. Separate drones are planned for terrain mapping, LiDAR/DEM and forest-type analysis.

No stated plan exists for who turns that imagery into orthomosaics, canopy models, thermal detections or LiDAR products. Mongabay India (August 2024) identifies data-processing time and technical know-how as principal barriers to ecological drone use in India, and notes Project Nilgiri Tahr thermal drones failed in daytime when rock temperature exceeded animal body temperature, a data-quality problem needing analytic correction.

**Caveat:** no official statement of a "backlog" was found. This is strongly inferred from the pilot-training-only model plus the documented processing barrier.

**First project.** An open, reproducible pipeline: OpenDroneMap photogrammetry to orthomosaic/DEM, thermal-anomaly detection, LiDAR canopy-height workflow. Demonstrate on any legally obtained sample data, then offer it to a forest circle or tiger-reserve foundation.

### Gap 3: Vernacular, offline-first tools for frontline staff

M-STrIPES (NTCA/WII, launched 2010, mandatory across all tiger reserves) is Android-based and does store data offline for later upload. But its design assumes literate, English-comfortable operators. There is no widely adopted, fully vernacular, icon-driven, voice-enabled tool for Tamil, Kannada or Malayalam speaking forest watchers. Pure software, no hardware, and the users are at Sathyamangalam where you can meet them.

### Gap 4: Bioacoustics coverage for Indian species

- BirdNET was trained overwhelmingly on North American and European species (Kahl et al., 2021, covering 984 species). A 2025 global assessment in Ecological Indicators confirms Africa and Asia perform consistently worse.
- BirdCLEF 2024 was built around under-studied Western Ghats birds, linked to V.V. Robin's lab at IISER Tirupati. The gap is real and actively worked, but far from solved.
- Amphibians are worse. There is no non-commercial multi-species frog classifier for India. The first open frog classifier (VicFrogNET) is Australian.
- **Chainsaw and gunshot acoustic detection has no published deployment inside Indian forests**, despite being proven abroad (Rainforest Connection; open AudioMoth + CNN work in Belize). Closest Indian analogue is Indian Railways' fibre-optic Distributed Acoustic Sensing for elephant-train collisions, a different use case.
- SACON Anaikatty (on your doorstep) has active bioacoustics work: Divyapriya and Pramod on Anaikatty Hills soundscapes, Shirish Manchi on cave swiftlets.

**First project.** A Western Ghats frog-call or Eastern Ghats bird classifier from your own AudioMoth recordings plus Xeno-canto data, open-sourced with a WILDLABS write-up. Or a low-cost chainsaw-detection proof of concept.

### Gap 5: Camera-trap AI beyond tigers

India's CaTRAT (WII, used in All India Tiger Estimation) and ExtractCompare (stripe-pattern individual ID) are strong on tigers. MegaDetector is strong on detection. Species-level classification for small and cryptic Indian fauna (rusty-spotted cat, pangolin, civets, mouse deer) is weak, and first-reserve records keep turning up accidentally during tiger counts. TrailGuard AI (RESOLVE/Nightjar, in Kanha-Pench and Dudhwa since 2022) shows appetite for on-device inference.

### Gap 6: Human-elephant conflict prediction, not detection

The Coimbatore-Sathyamangalam-Valparai corridor is the most instrumented conflict landscape in India, which cuts both ways.
- NCF's Valparai Elephant Information Network (from 2011): bulk SMS in Tamil and English to up to 1,500 people, missed-call-activated red beacons, local TV crawls. NCF research found 36 of 41 elephant-related deaths in Valparai since 1994 could have been prevented with a warning.
- TN Forest Department has AI thermal/IR camera command centres (46 cameras at Nadugani, Gudalur; camera points chosen from 10 years of detection data).

**Detection is crowded. Prediction is not.** Forecasting where a herd will be tomorrow from movement history, crop calendars, water and moon phase is an open data-science problem. Best done with NCF or the forest department, not alone.

### Gap 7: Compartment-level forest fire prediction

FSI provides near-real-time MODIS/VIIRS detection and a weekly, zone-level Fire Weather Index on the Van Agni geoportal. It does not provide compartment-level, locally parameterised daily fire-risk prediction. Indian research is filling this in the Himalayas and central India (Singh and Jeganathan, BIT Mesra, random forest over MP and Chhattisgarh, Advances in Space Research 2024, explicitly noting extension to the Western Ghats). Tamil Nadu and the Western Ghats are comparatively under-served.

## 9. Non-Obvious Entry Mechanics

1. **Open-source contribution as a credential.** Technology for Wildlife's GitHub, MegaDetector / Microsoft AI for Earth, Wildlife Insights, Wildbook/Wild Me, BirdNET, Rainforest Connection, OpenForis. A merged pull request beats a certificate with these employers.
2. **WILDLABS.net.** Global conservation-tech community, roughly 6,000 people in 120 countries, groups on camera traps, AI, sensors, bioacoustics. Careers board and awards. Very few Indians active. One good technical thread makes you visible.
3. **Citizen science as credibility.** Systematic contribution to eBird India, iNaturalist India, SeasonWatch, Frog Watch builds a public naturalist track record that partly offsets the lack of a biology degree.
4. **Volunteer into protected areas.** All India Tiger Estimation phase-1 volunteer surveys, state forest-department census volunteering (Anamalai and others use M-STrIPES-based counts partly staffed by volunteers), NGO and research-station volunteer schemes (Keystone, Agumbe, IISER Tirupati via the YETI mailing list).
5. **Vendor and consultancy route, with a real caveat.** Procurement runs via tntenders.gov.in (needs a Class-3 Digital Signature Certificate), the Central Public Procurement Portal, and GeM. Many state RFPs carry domain-turnover clauses that exclude a new studio (a representative forest RFP required Rs 50 lakh annual turnover for three years plus profitability). **The lever:** register as an Udyam MSME and a DPIIT-recognised startup. On GeM this unlocks EMD exemption, waiver of turnover and prior-experience benchmarks, and L1-plus-15% price matching. MSMEs accounted for roughly 47% of GeM order value in FY2025-26. Realistic near-term entry is GeM services plus subcontracting under a PMU, not winning a large RFP outright.

### Parallel exam track (only if permanence becomes the priority again)

- Key insight: you do not have to enter the forest department as a tech person. Enter as a Forester and become the tech person inside it. Departments are short of staff who can run GIS, drones, camera-trap pipelines and M-STrIPES, and such staff get pulled into monitoring cells and tiger reserve research wings.

## 12. Caveats and Unverified Claims

- **Pay and permanence.** NGO research-associate and project-assistant roles pay modestly and are contract-based, tied to funding cycles. Government project posts (JRF, Project Associate) are fixed-term. TNGCC young-professional pay could not be verified officially (an unofficial aggregator suggested Rs 30,000 to 50,000/month; treat as unverified). The field-based life is real in these roles but comes with income insecurity for the first few years.
- **Degree ceilings remain.** Permanent government scientific posts, ACF and Range Officer, and most WII/SACON academic tracks require postgraduate qualifications and often exams. Rufford will not fund a BSc-only individual directly. The Habitats Trust Conservation Grant is organisation-only.
- **Competition.** WILDLABS, National Geographic, Mohamed bin Zayed and Rufford are internationally competitive. The edge is hyper-local specificity plus real CS skills. Keep proposals concrete and place-based.
- **Not verified:** an official statement of a drone data-processing backlog (strongly inferred, not stated). No currently-open TN, Karnataka or Kerala forest tender specifically for camera-trap AI, drone processing or invasive mapping was confirmed. No published chainsaw or gunshot acoustic deployment was found inside Indian forests, which supports the niche claim but is not absolute proof none exists.
- **Reconfirm before acting** on any date, fee, vacancy count or eligibility line in this document. Notifications change and corrigenda are frequent.
