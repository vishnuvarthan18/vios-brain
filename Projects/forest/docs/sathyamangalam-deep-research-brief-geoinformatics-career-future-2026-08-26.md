For Vishnu to run in a separate Claude Cowork session. **Purpose: this is bigger than the Sathyamangalam atlas project. Before committing to geoinformatics/conservation technology as a life-long career path — not just this one project — I need to know if the field itself has real, growing demand, backed by hard numbers, and what the actual entry path looks like. This is two separate questions answered in one report: (1) is the field growing, with evidence, and (2) if it is, what is the real path in for a CS graduate.** Do not produce generic "green jobs are the future" commentary — I need verifiable numbers or an honest "no solid data exists" for each claim.

## Depth and format requirements — read before starting

- **Every growth claim needs a number, a year, and a source.** "Increasingly important" is not acceptable. If a number can't be found, say "no solid data found" rather than estimating one.
- **Separate the field-level question from the personal-path question completely.** Task 1–3 answer whether the field has a future. Task 4–6 answer how a specific person gets into it. Do not blend them.
- **India and global should be reported side by side wherever comparable data exists**, so it's clear whether this is an India-specific opportunity, a global one, or bigger in one than the other.
- Use tables for anything comparable across years, countries, or programs.
- Every section ends with its own sources list.

## Context to paste in

I am a CS graduate based in Erode, Tamil Nadu, India, currently building a landscape-scale conservation data project (a "biodiversity atlas" for a tiger reserve, combining species data, historical documents, legal records and a disputed-facts feature). I'm now asking a much bigger question: should I pursue "geoinformatics + forestry/conservation" as my actual long-term career and field of study — not just for this one reserve, but across India, and potentially internationally? I want real evidence this field is growing before committing years to it, and if it is, I want to know the actual entry route: degrees, certifications, jobs, and realistic pay — not marketing copy from university websites.

## What I need, strictly in this order

### Task 1 (do first) — Is "geoinformatics for forestry/conservation" an actual growing field? Hard numbers only.
Find real data, not sentiment, on:
- Market size and growth rate for geospatial/GIS technology specifically applied to forestry, conservation, and environmental monitoring — global market reports (Grand View Research, MarketsandMarkets, etc.) with actual figures and CAGR, cited by name and year.
- India-specific: government spending and programme scale on remote sensing/GIS for forestry and conservation — ISRO/NRSC/Bhuvan budget or programme scale, Forest Survey of India's technology budget, any Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) digital/GIS initiatives with actual rupee figures where findable.
- Job posting volume and trend — search Indian job platforms (Naukri, LinkedIn India) and global ones for actual counts of postings mentioning "GIS forestry," "remote sensing conservation," "geoinformatics wildlife," etc., over the last 2–3 years if historical data is retrievable, to see direction not just a snapshot.
- Any published forecast (government, UN, World Bank, industry report) for demand in environmental geospatial roles specifically, for the next 5–10 years.

### Task 2 — Who is actually hiring for this, and at what scale?
- Name real employers in India that hire for this combination specifically: government bodies (ISRO/NRSC, Forest Departments, WII, Zoological/Botanical Survey of India), NGOs (WWF-India, WTI, Nature Conservation Foundation, ATREE), private geospatial firms (Esri India, RMSI, SatSure, etc.), and any conservation-tech startups.
- For each, find actual open roles right now if possible (job board snapshots), typical qualifications required, and salary ranges where disclosed (Glassdoor, AmbitionBox, Payscale India, or job listings themselves).
- Do the same for global remote/international opportunities: conservation tech organisations that hire remotely or sponsor international staff (e.g. WCS, WWF international offices, Wildlife Conservation Society, SMART Partnership, Esri Conservation Program, GBIF secretariat, IUCN), and note which actually employ India-based or remote workers versus requiring relocation.
- Compare: is the volume of real opportunity bigger in government, NGO, or private sector — in India specifically?

### Task 3 — What does the money actually look like, realistically, at different career stages?
- Entry-level, mid-level and senior salary ranges for GIS/remote-sensing/conservation-data roles in India, with sources (not guesses).
- Same for equivalent international/remote roles, converted to a comparable figure, so it's clear whether going global is worth it financially versus staying in India.
- Freelance/consulting rate data if any exists for this niche (e.g. ecological GIS consulting, geospatial analysis contracts).

### Task 4 — Rank real geoinformatics/remote-sensing degree and certification programs, India and global
- List and rank actual programs (not just "IIRS exists") by: cost, duration, whether it's online/remote-friendly, entry requirements, and — critically — **documented job placement outcomes or graduate destinations** where published.
- Include at minimum: IIRS (Indian Institute of Remote Sensing, ISRO) courses and their actual duration/cost/eligibility; relevant M.Tech/M.Sc programs at Indian universities (Anna University, Andhra University, IIRS-Andhra University joint programs, IIT/IISc geoinformatics offerings if they exist); international options realistic for a working Indian professional (online M.Sc in GIS from a recognised university, Esri/Coursera/edX certifications with actual industry recognition, not just completion certificates).
- Flag which of these can be done part-time/remotely while working, versus requiring full-time relocation.

### Task 5 — What do people who actually did this say?
- Find real accounts (LinkedIn posts, blog posts, forum threads — Reddit r/gis, r/india, professional forums) from people who transitioned from a general CS/tech background into GIS/remote-sensing/conservation tech, specifically in India if possible. What was their actual path, and did it pay off?
- Any documented cases of failure or regret — people who studied this and could not find relevant work — to balance the picture.

### Task 6 — Synthesis: does this justify a life-long commitment, and what's the realistic first move?
- Based on Tasks 1–5, give a direct verdict: is this a field with real, growing, fundable demand — or a niche that looks exciting but has thin real opportunity? State the confidence level and what evidence it rests on.
- If the verdict is positive: what is the single best next concrete step (a specific course, certification, or credential) to start now, that doesn't require quitting current plans, and roughly what timeline to a first paid opportunity in this space.
- If mixed: what conditions would need to be true for it to be worth committing to full-time.
