# Engineering Mastery Roadmap

## Context
No formal engineering degree. Goal: become excellent across software engineering, electronics/EE, mechanical engineering, and broader tech/product strategy — in that priority order — plus sales (parallel, always-on) and engineering thinking (cross-cutting reasoning layer, not a sequenced subject).

## Why (motivation)
Not skill-collecting for its own sake — conviction that software, electronics, and mechanical engineering are foundational to where the future is heading (AI-driven products increasingly need people who understand the full stack: software, hardware, and the physical systems they connect to). Going deep now is a deliberate bet on relevance, not a hobby. Sales and engineering-thinking were added because raw domain knowledge isn't the whole goal — being genuinely excellent means also knowing how to reason like an engineer and how to sell/advocate for what you build.

## Priority order (sequenced)
1. Software engineering
2. Electronics / EE
3. Mechanical engineering
4. Broader tech/product strategy

## Parallel / cross-cutting (not sequenced)
- **Sales** — runs continuously alongside whichever sequenced subject is active. Light, always-on (~30-60 min/week), not a dedicated block.
- **Engineering thinking** — the reasoning layer applied *inside* every domain (first-principles, systems thinking, constraints, debugging mindset, tradeoff analysis). Not a subject to "finish."

## Time budget
7–10 hrs/week total. Mostly weighted to the active sequenced subject (currently software), with a small slice always going to sales.

## Process per domain
1. Conversational diagnostic quiz in chat — gauge actual current level.
2. Record calibrated level (beginner / intermediate / advanced, with specifics on gaps).
3. Build a learning roadmap matched to that level.
4. Track progress over time in that domain's doc.
