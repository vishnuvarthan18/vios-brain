## Roadmap

### Phase 1 — Physical fundamentals (start here)
- Force, mass, weight, torque — intuitive understanding, not just formulas
- Basic statics: how forces balance in a structure (why a bridge doesn't collapse)
- Simple machines: levers, gears, pulleys, inclined planes — and why they matter to every mechanism
- Units and orders of magnitude (a practical gap many self-taught learners skip)

### Phase 2 — Materials
- Common materials and their properties: steel, aluminum, plastics, composites
- Strength, stiffness, weight tradeoffs — why a material is chosen for a given part
- Basic failure modes: what makes something break (fatigue, stress concentration)

### Phase 3 — CAD and design
- Learn one CAD tool properly (e.g. Fusion 360 — free, beginner-friendly, industry-relevant)
- Model simple parts and assemblies
- Basic tolerancing and fits — why parts are designed to specific dimensions, not "close enough"

### Phase 4 — Manufacturing processes
- How things are actually made: machining, injection molding, 3D printing, sheet metal, casting
- Design for manufacturing (DFM) — why a design choice matters once it has to be produced at scale
- Cost and tradeoff intuition: why one process is chosen over another

### Phase 5 — Structural analysis and mechanisms
- Basic stress/strain analysis, intro to FEA (finite element analysis) concepts
- Mechanism design: linkages, motion transfer, actuators
- Where this connects back to electronics — robotics and embedded hardware sit at this intersection
