# Full Multi-Year Plan — Timeline View

Consolidated from all 6 domain docs. This is the "how it all fits together over time" view — the individual docs hold the phase-level detail.

## End state (the target)
Someone who can take an idea from physical hardware + embedded software through to a working product, understand and reason about every layer of it (mechanical, electronics, code), evaluate whether it's actually a good bet, and sell/pitch it convincingly — without needing five specialists in the room. Realistic category: deep-tech/hardware-software founder or independent builder of AI-connected physical products.

## Overall shape
- 7–10 hrs/week total, sequenced priority (software → electronics → mechanical → strategy), with sales running parallel throughout at low intensity, and engineering-thinking applied inside every phase of every subject — not learned separately.
- Timeline below is a starting estimate, not a fixed schedule. It MUST be tuned against your actual speed — see "Speed tracking" below. This is the single most important operating rule of this plan.

## Speed tracking — how the timeline actually gets tuned
The Year 1/2/3 timeline is calibrated to a hypothetical average learner at 7–10 hrs/week. You are not hypothetical, so the timeline is expected to drift, and that's normal, not failure.

- **Checkpoint every 4–6 weeks:** compare what you've actually completed in the active domain's progress log against what the plan assumed for that period. Faster than planned, slower than planned, or on pace — say so, in that domain's doc.
- **If you're consistently faster** (a phase that should take 4 months takes 2): pull the next phase forward, don't just idle at the planned pace. Update the milestone months in this doc down.
- **If you're consistently slower** (missed hours most weeks, or a phase is taking noticeably longer): don't panic-compress later phases. Push the milestone months out honestly, and check whether the 7–10 hrs/week assumption itself is realistic right now — if actual weekly hours are lower, the timeline should reflect that, not pretend.
- **Signal to watch, not just hours:** speed isn't only about time spent — it's whether a phase's core skill actually landed (e.g. can you predict a circuit's behavior, not just did you spend the hours). Moving on before a phase has actually landed is what makes later phases feel impossibly slow.
- **Recalibrate this doc directly** when a checkpoint shows real drift — update the Year/Month milestones below rather than letting this doc go stale while the domain docs move on without it.

## Year 1 — Foundations (Software primary, Electronics starts)
**Months 1–4: Software Phase 1–2**
- Programming fundamentals (variables, loops, functions) → HTML/CSS/web basics → correct mental model of frontend/backend/API/database
- Sales: light parallel practice (explaining your own work, basic persuasion reading)

**Months 4–7: Software Phase 3–4**
- Reading real code, debugging, proper Git, rebuild a vibe-coded project by hand
- Software goal (read/debug/direct AI code fluently) substantially reached by end of this stretch

**Months 7–12: Electronics Phase 1–2 begins**
- Electrical fundamentals (voltage/current/resistance/Ohm's law), breadboard practice, multimeter use
- Software shifts to maintenance mode — light ongoing practice, not primary focus anymore

**Milestone at 12 months:** Comfortable reading/writing basic code and directing AI tools with real understanding. Can explain and predict simple circuit behavior instead of copying tutorials.

## Year 2 — Electronics depth, Mechanical starts
**Months 12–18: Electronics Phase 3 (embedded systems)**
- Microcontrollers properly understood, C/C++ for embedded, I2C/SPI/UART, power basics

**Months 18–24: Electronics Phase 4 (PCB design) + Mechanical Phase 1–2 begins**
- KiCad, schematic capture, first fabricated PCB
- Mechanical fundamentals: force/torque/statics, materials basics

**Milestone at 24 months:** Can design and get a simple PCB made. Mechanical fundamentals in place — not yet building physical mechanisms, but reasoning about materials/forces correctly.

## Year 3 — Mechanical depth, Strategy formalizes
**Months 24–30: Mechanical Phase 3–4**
- CAD fluency (Fusion 360), manufacturing processes, design-for-manufacturing thinking

**Months 30–36: Mechanical Phase 5 + Strategy Phase 1–3**
- Structural analysis basics, mechanism design — this is where electronics + mechanical start meeting (robotics/embedded hardware)
- Strategy: hype-vs-substance evaluation framework, competitive analysis, business models broadened beyond SaaS

**Milestone at 36 months:** Can take a product idea from CAD model + PCB + embedded code to a working physical prototype. Can evaluate whether a tech product/company is substance or hype with a real framework.

## Years 3–5 — Specialization, Strategy/Sales mature
- Pick an electronics/mechanical specialization lane (robotics, IoT product design, power electronics — whichever pulls you by then)
- Strategy Phase 4–5: advising/influence skills, hardware strategy specifically (ties directly into what you've built hands-on by now)
- Sales: from "explaining your own projects" to real pitches — investors, partners, early customers, as actual opportunities arise
- Engineering-thinking mental models should be second nature by now, not something you consciously reference

**Milestone at ~5 years:** The end-state profile described above — someone who can conceive, design, build, and pitch a hardware-software product end to end, credibly.

## How to read this plan
- Timeline shifts with actual weekly consistency — 7-10 hrs/week is the assumption. Less time, longer timeline; more time (esp. once a subject "clicks" and becomes enjoyable), faster.
- Sequencing is a guide, not a cage. If electronics starts pulling harder than expected, or a real project demands mechanical earlier, follow that — the priority order was your call at setup, not a hard rule.
- Review and recalibrate this timeline roughly every 6 months against the individual domain progress logs — and every 4–6 weeks at the lighter checkpoint level described in "Speed tracking" above.
