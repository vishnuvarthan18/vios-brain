**Root cause chain:** Middle East energy shock → oil +37% → global inflation re-accelerates →
central banks stop easing → capital leaves emerging markets → rupee weak → imported inflation
in India. Nearly everything else is downstream of the oil price. Watch Brent.

**Key correction to the popular narrative:** India's demand indicators are accelerating —
86% of 50 leading indicators up in Q1 FY27, bank credit +17.7%, PV sales +24%, GST e-way
bills +14.5%. The squeeze is on prices and specific sectors, not on business broadly.

### Indian IT — the structural break

- Indian tech revenue: **$315B in FY26, +6.1%**. Workforce: **5.95 million, +2.3%**.
  Revenue and headcount have **decoupled** — the thirty-year "more revenue = more engineers"
  equation is over.
- Industry AI revenue $10–12B; TCS alone at ~$1.8B annualised run rate.
- TCS cutting 12,000+ (≈2% of headcount); reported totals through 2026 exceed 23,000 across
  the sector. This is margin-and-automation restructuring, not demand collapse — TCS still
  hired 9,279 freshers in Q1 FY27.

## 3. GCCs — the structural alternative

A **Global Capability Centre** is a multinational's own in-house office in India (Walmart
Global Tech, JPMorgan, Target, Shell, Goldman Sachs, Airbus, Novartis). Direct employment by
the parent, working on their real product. No billing rate attached to your head, no bench.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| GCCs in India | ~2,120 centres |
| 2026 hiring | ~510,000 roles projected; 3.4× since 2021 |
| H1 2026 hires | 228,000, +11% y/y |
| Attrition | 9% — lowest of any tech segment |
| Global leadership roles now in Indian GCCs | 6,500+ |
| Pay vs IT services | 25–40% higher; 40–80% at senior levels |
| Roles requiring AI / data / automation skills | 64% |

**Function mix:** IT & software dev 31% · AI, data science & analytics 18% (fastest growing,
+38% y/y) · engineering & product R&D 16% · cloud & cybersecurity 11%.

**Cities:** Bengaluru 30% · Hyderabad 15% · Pune 12% · Mumbai 11% · tier-2 (Coimbatore,
Jaipur, Kochi) 15% and growing 23% y/y with lower attrition (8–12% vs 18–22% metro).

**Experience mix:** 4–10 years is 56% of hiring; 0–3 years is 30% and growing fastest (+18%).

### The maturity curve — which stage matters more than the brand

1. **Cost centre** — support and maintenance, work handed down. Avoid; as AI-exposed as services.
2. **Delivery centre** — owns modules, not the roadmap. Acceptable entry point.
3. **Capability centre** — owns products and technical direction. The sweet spot.
4. **Transformation hub** — leads global functions from India. Where the 6,500 global roles sit.

### Real downsides

Cost-line on someone else's balance sheet (parent's bad year = review of the India centre);
stage-1/2 centres are not safe; slower pace and enterprise politics; hiring is referral and
LinkedIn-outreach driven, not job boards; timezone tax for US-parent centres.

## 4. Design and design-engineering — the 2026 read

### What's commoditizing

- NN/g's State of UX 2026: design systems plus AI tools mean **anyone can produce a decent
  looking UI**. The visual-craft tier is losing its price premium.
- Agency/freelance website work is the most AI-exposed segment in this whole document.
- Vibe coding is **not a moat** — 76% of designers use AI coding tools, PMs and engineers are
  now producing interfaces too.

### What's rising

- **43% of companies now require working prototypes**, not static mockups, as a design deliverable.
- **~50% of designers have shipped AI-generated code to production**, many without a design
  engineer title. Adoption: 68% at early-stage companies, 33% at public companies.
- 53% report improved job satisfaction from AI; designers who build report feeling twice as
  creative and capable.
- Designers increasingly own the quality guardrails as non-designers generate more interfaces.

### What holds value

80% of designers say their judgment on quality and polish is irreplaceable. The industry line
that matters: *the hard part of design is rarely generating the form — it's understanding the
problem well enough.*

The durable four: **problem definition · taste and creative direction · systems thinking ·
domain depth.** Plus verification — being the person who can tell whether output is correct.

### Three viable futures — options discussed, none chosen

1. **Design engineer** (recommended) — product designer who ships production front-end.
   Structurally scarce because the two skill sets rarely co-occur. Already ~60% there.
2. **AI product designer** — designing trust, transparency, control and failure states for AI
   systems. Barely a defined discipline; whoever gets good now defines it.
3. **Solo builder / founder** — design + vibe coding makes end-to-end solo shipping possible.
   Grow into it after 3–4 years of seeing real products fail.

## 5. The plan

### Next 12 months

- **Ship two real products end-to-end** — designed and built solo, deployed, with real users
  however few. Not portfolio concepts.
- **Write up the decisions**, not the screens: what was cut, what was wrong, what surprised.
  A portfolio of decisions beats a portfolio of screens.
- **Get properly good at React and one design system** — beyond tutorial level.
- **Pick one domain and stay in it** (whatever the current employer's clients are in).
- **Learn to run a basic usability test.** Research is the skill most designers skip.
- Guard against drifting into being a mediocre engineer — the design judgment half is the
  harder half to rebuild.

### The longer arc

- **24–26 · Build the exit profile.** One stack deep, one domain understood at business level,
  two shipped products, six months of expenses saved so the move is a choice not a rescue.
- **26–29 · Jump and go deep.** Stage-3 GCC or product company. Expect a 40–80% comp step.
  Then stay 3–4 years — depth compounds; job-hopping for 20% bumps undoes it.
- **29–34 · Choose the ladder deliberately.** Technical depth (staff/principal, architect) vs
  leadership (EM, then global roles). Failure mode is drifting into management by default.
- **34+ · Optionality.** Domain authority, a network that returns calls, shipped systems.

### Money rules

- Six months of expenses liquid — not three. Layoff risk in the sector is real.
- Own health insurance separate from the employer policy; cheapest to buy now.
- **No big EMI in the next 18 months.** Fixed obligations turn a job loss into a crisis. This
  is the highest-leverage financial decision available and it's a decision to *not* do something.
- Don't chase gold at $4,342 or silver at $63 — record-high fear assets after the move.
- Expect low single-digit hikes in services against 5–6% living-cost inflation.
- Not financial advice; general principles only.
