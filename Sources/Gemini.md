Social class is typically divided into 
five main tiers
 based on income, wealth, education, occupation, and economic security:
Lower Class:
 Individuals or households with limited financial stability, often relying on hourly wages, part-time work, or public assistance. Financial buffers are minimal or non-existent, making unexpected expenses (like medical emergencies) critical risks.
Lower Middle Class:
 White-collar support staff, skilled trade workers, or entry-level professionals. They earn enough to cover basic needs and modest savings, but face high vulnerability to economic downturns or inflation.
Middle Class:
 Established professionals, managers, and specialized technicians. They typically own homes, afford comfortable living standards, fund higher education for children, and maintain steady retirement savings.
Upper Middle Class:
 High-earning salaried professionals, senior managers, doctors, and specialized consultants. They enjoy significant disposable income, high-value assets, substantial investments, and considerable financial freedom.
Upper Class:
 Wealth and asset holders (investors, major business owners, inherited wealth). Their primary source of income is capital and investments rather than labor or salary.
How Social Class Is Defined
Sociologists and economists define these tiers using a combination of quantitative metrics and qualitative factors rather than a single fixed number:
Income vs. Wealth:
 Income measures what you earn annually, while wealth measures what you own minus what you owe (net worth, property, stocks, savings). True class mobility and security are usually dictated by wealth, not just a paycheck.
Economic Security & Predictability:
 The ability to absorb financial shocks without falling into debt. Lower tiers live paycheck-to-paycheck; middle and upper tiers have emergency reserves and safety nets.
Occupation & Autonomy:
 The type of work performed (manual vs. intellectual) and the degree of control an individual has over their time and daily labor.
Cultural and Social Capital:
 Educational credentials, professional networks, and lifestyle habits that influence social mobility and access to opportunities.
