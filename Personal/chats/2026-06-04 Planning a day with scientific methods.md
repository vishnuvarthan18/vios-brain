**Claude:** ## Why routines work

Three mechanisms with solid research behind them:

- **Decision fatigue** — willpower and decision-making quality degrade through the day (Baumeister, 1998). A routine removes dozens of micro-decisions, preserving mental energy for what matters.
- **Habit loops** — MIT research showed the brain converts repeated sequences into automatic patterns, reducing cognitive load to near zero once a routine is ingrained.
- **Implementation intentions** — Gollwitzer's studies show that pre-deciding *when* and *where* you'll do something increases follow-through by 2–3x versus just intending to do it.

## How to build one that sticks

**1. Anchor to fixed points, not times**
Build your routine around things that already happen — waking up, eating, finishing work — not arbitrary clock times. "After I make coffee, I review my day" beats "9:15am: review day."

**2. Time-block your day into three energy zones**
- Morning (peak): deep work, decisions, creative tasks
- Midday (trough): admin, email, meetings
- Afternoon (rebound): lighter creative, planning tomorrow

**3. Pick 1–3 Most Important Tasks the night before**
Write them down. Your first hour should be spent only on those. Nothing else until they're done or genuinely blocked.

**4. Use habit stacking for personal routines**
Chain new habits to existing ones: exercise, journaling, reading all attach to existing anchors. Don't schedule them in isolation.

**5. Protect transition time**
15 minutes between major blocks. No back-to-back commitments. Context-switching has a measurable recovery cost (~23 minutes per interruption per Gloria Mark's research).

**Start small:** Design one morning block that works. Run it for two weeks before adding more structure. Complexity kills new routines.

**Claude:** ## Governing Science (3 principles shaping every decision)

- **Circadian alignment** — cortisol peaks 30–45 min after waking, ideal for physical output. Cognitive peak hits ~2 hours after waking, best for learning and deep work. Afternoon trough (13:00–15:00) is the worst time for hard thinking — use it for admin only.
- **Ultradian rhythm** — the brain naturally works in 90-minute focus cycles. All deep work blocks are structured to this.
- **Implementation intentions** — Gollwitzer's research: pre-assigning *what, when, where* increases follow-through by 2–3x over vague goals. Every block below is a locked intention, not a suggestion.

## Cycling Protocol for Weight Loss

Zone 2 fasted cardio is the most evidence-backed fat-loss method for beginners — you can hold a conversation, HR roughly 120–135 bpm. High intensity at this stage increases appetite and injury risk without proportional fat loss.

- **Weeks 1–2:** 3 days/week (Mon, Wed, Fri), 30 min each
- **Weeks 3–4:** 5 days/week, 30 min
- **Week 5 onward:** Add 5 min per week until you reach 60 min on weekdays

Do not skip the fasted condition — eat after, not before.

---

## Sleep (The Non-Negotiable Foundation)

5:30 wake + 23:00 sleep = 6.5 hours. Minimum viable, not optimal. If you feel chronically fatigued after 2 weeks, shift sleep to 22:30 and adjust the evening block to 20:30–22:00.

Two hard rules:
- Phone out of the bedroom by 22:30 — this is almost certainly what's pushing your current sleep past midnight
- **Same wake time every day including weekends** — this single habit is the highest-impact sleep intervention known. Varying it by even 90 min destroys circadian stability
