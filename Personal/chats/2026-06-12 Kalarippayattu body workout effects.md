Start light. A wooden gada like yours is the right beginner tool. The core movement is the **360 swing**; learn it in stages.

**Setup**
- Stand feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, core braced.
- Grip both hands together at the bottom of the handle (the thin end), stacked. Hold the gada vertical in front of your chest — this is the **rack position**.
- If your left hand is above your right, you push the mace ball over your right shoulder; let it swing behind your back, and when it reaches your left shoulder, pull it back over to the front.

**Progression — don't skip steps**

1. **Pendulum first.** Let the mace head drop behind your back and swing it side to side like a pendulum, hands held behind your head. This teaches the back path and the weight feel before you attempt a full circle.
2. **360 swing.** From rack, push the head over one shoulder, let it fall behind your back, and catch/pull it back to the front rack on the other side. That's one full circle around your head.
3. **Switch directions.** Then swap your hand stacking and swing the other way.

**Key technique points**
- Let momentum do the work on the back side of the swing — don't death-grip the handle. Keep shoulders relaxed behind the head, but core tight throughout.
- Rotation comes from your hips and upper back, not from yanking with the arms.
- Keep your eyes forward and the mace path close to your body. The heavy head stays behind you; control the catch.

**Safety**
- Train where the head can't hit anything if you lose grip — open space, away from your own head.
- Start with low reps (5–8 each direction), focus on smooth control over speed.
- Wrists and shoulders fatigue fast at first; stop when form breaks down.

Here are the main **karlakattai / club types**, sorted by what suits a beginner. Your wooden gada (the one you uploaded) is a single-headed mace — good, but heavier and harder to control than the entry-level options.

**Main types**

- **Kai karlai (கை கரலை)** — arm-length single club, swung one- or two-handed. Weight is chosen based on your bodyweight, and it has 64 primary movements (sutrus) forming a complete strength-endurance regime. This is the foundational Tamil club. Best all-rounder.
- **Jori / Jori** — pair of large bottle-shaped clubs (one in each hand), swung behind the shoulders. Heavier, two-club coordination — intermediate+.
- **Mugdar / Mudgar** — Indian club with a rounded head on a handle; classic akhara strength tool. Comes light to very heavy.
- **Gada (your photo)** — single mace, weight concentrated at the far end on a long handle. Longest lever, hardest to control. A short Hanuman Gada (50–90 cm) qualifies as a "heavy club."
- **Meel (Persian)** — teardrop-shaped clubs, usually paired; circular-swing focused. Different lineage but often grouped in.

**Choosing — my recommendation**

Start with a **kai karlai or a light mugdar / short club**, not the long gada. Reasons:

- 4–5 kg in wood is the standard beginner recommendation for heavy clubs.
- Shorter handle = shorter lever = far more control and lower injury risk while you learn the swing path.
- A single club swung one- or two-handed lets you do a huge variety of suttrus without needing complex moves to benefit.

Your long gada is better as a **second tool** once your 360 and grip are solid — its long lever multiplies both the benefit and the risk.
